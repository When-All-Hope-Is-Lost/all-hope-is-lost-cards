## Do this now
If officials say leave, leave early. Take your go-bag, medicines and pets, and follow the official routes.

## Steps
1. **Plan ahead:** Know your risks, evacuation routes, and where you can go if you need to leave. Check your local emergency management website.
2. **Make a go-bag:** Pack a bag with supplies you may need, like water, food, medications, flashlights, and a first aid kit.
3. **Know where to go:** Choose a place to stay, like a friend’s house or a shelter. Know more than one place in case your first choice is not available.
4. **Prepare for pets:** If you have pets, plan how to care for them. Most public shelters only allow service animals, so find a place that will accept your pets.
5. **Keep your car ready:** Keep a full tank of gas if you think you may need to leave. Keep a half tank of gas in your car at all times.
6. **Follow instructions:** Listen to local officials and follow their directions. Do not take shortcuts or drive through flooded areas.
7. **Secure your home:** Close and lock doors and windows. Unplug small electronics, but leave freezers and refrigerators plugged in unless there is a risk of flooding.
8. **Leave a note** saying when you left and where you're going, and tell an out-of-area contact.
9. **Dress for it:** sturdy shoes, long pants, long sleeves, a hat. Check on neighbors who may need a ride.
10. **Your route may be on foot.** Know other ways out, including public transportation.

## If / then
- **If** you are told to evacuate **then** leave early enough to avoid being trapped by severe weather.
- **If** you have a car **then** keep a full tank of gas and a portable emergency kit in your car.
- **If** you do not have a car **then** plan with family, friends, or your local emergency management office for help.
- **If** you have pets **then** plan where they will stay and make sure they are with you during the evacuation.
- **If** you are returning home after an evacuation **then** check with officials both where you are and back home before you travel. Stay away from downed power lines and never run a generator indoors or in a garage.

## Don't
- Don't wait until the last minute to leave.
- Don't drive through flooded areas.
- Don't take shortcuts that may be blocked or unsafe.
- Don't ignore local officials' instructions.
- Don't count on getting gas. Stations may be closed or have no power.

## Get help now
- **911**: immediate danger.
- **Check local emergency management websites** for shelter locations and updates.
- **Download the FEMA app** for real-time alerts and shelter information.
