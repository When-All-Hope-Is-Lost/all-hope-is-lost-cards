## Do this now
Take five minutes for yourself: slow, deep breaths, then talk to one person you trust about how you're feeling.

## Steps
1. **Take a break from news and social media.** Constant bad news is upsetting. Check once a day from a trusted source.
2. **Make time to unwind:** deep breathing, stretching, a walk outside, or something small you enjoy.
3. **Take care of your body:** regular meals when you can, physical activity, and sleep. Avoid excessive alcohol, tobacco, and drugs.
4. **Connect with others:** talk with people you trust, and reach out to community or faith groups. Helping a neighbor can lift you too.
5. **Write it down:** a short journal, or a list of things you're grateful for, can help you sort through feelings.
6. **Shrink the problem:** pick the next one or two steps from your plan, not all of them.

## If / then
- **If** stress, sadness, or worry gets in the way of daily life for several days **then** reach out to a health care provider or counselor (community health centers charge on a sliding scale; see the medication card).
- **If** worry lasts for months and is hard to control **then** get help from a professional. It may be an anxiety disorder, and it's treatable.
- **If** the stress is about a disaster or emergency **then** call or text the Disaster Distress Helpline: **1-800-985-5990**.
- **If** you have thoughts of suicide or of hurting someone **then** open the crisis card now, or call or text **988**.

## Don't
- Don't try to carry everything alone.
- Don't lean on alcohol or drugs to cope (CDC advises avoiding excessive use).
- Don't doom-scroll. Take breaks from the news (CDC).

## Get help now
- **988**: call or text, or chat at chat.988lifeline.org.
- **Disaster Distress Helpline**: call or text 1-800-985-5990.
- **911**: immediate danger.
