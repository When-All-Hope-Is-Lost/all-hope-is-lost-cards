## Do this now
**Stay close, stay calm, and tell them you'll keep taking care of them.** Kids mostly need to feel **safe, connected, and loved**. If a child talks about wanting to die or hurt someone, call or text **988** now.

## Steps
1. **Be with them.** Spend extra time together, even short stretches. Offer gentle words, a hug if they want one, or just your presence.
2. **Young children (0–5):** lots of cuddling. Get down to their eye level and speak calmly in simple words. Take a breath first, and focus on them, not the event.
3. **Ask, then listen.** Ask what worries them and what might help. Most kids want to talk, so let them. Tell them it's **okay to feel sad, scared, or upset**, and that crying is okay.
4. **Let them express it other ways:** drawing, writing, play, music, or quiet time.
5. **Get back to routines** (meals, bedtime, school) as soon as you can. Give them small, age-appropriate jobs so they feel useful. You can excuse them from chores for a day or two first.
6. **Limit news and social media** about the event. Watch together when you can, and answer their questions honestly.
7. **Point out the helpers and the good**: people helping each other. Kids often feel better by helping, too, like writing thank-you notes.
8. **Take care of yourself.** Eat, sleep, and breathe. Kids take their cues from you. It's okay for them to see you sad, but try not to show intense anger or fear.

## If / then
- **If** you see regression (bedwetting, thumb-sucking), clinginess, new fears, sleep or eating changes, aches, acting out, or pulling away **then** know that these are **common at first**.
- **If** those reactions **last more than 2 to 4 weeks**, get worse, or show up later **then** talk with a doctor, school counselor, or mental health professional.
- **If** a teen says "I'm fine" but is withdrawn, arguing, or taking risks (alcohol or drugs) **then** check in gently and often, without pressure.
- **If** people are blaming a group for what happened **then** talk about not blaming whole groups of people, and tell your child that **they are not to blame**.

## Don't
- **Don't pressure kids to talk** or to join activities about the event. Let them step away.
- **Don't hit, isolate, abandon, or make fun of** a child who is struggling.
- Don't let talk about the event take over the whole day.

## Get help now
- **988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline:** call or text **988**, or chat at 988lifeline.org.
- **Disaster Distress Helpline:** call or text **1-800-985-5990** (press 2 for Spanish).
- **SAMHSA National Helpline:** **1-800-662-4357** (24/7 treatment referral).
- **Childhelp National Child Abuse Hotline:** call **1-800-422-4453**, or text **"GO"** to 800-422-4453 (24/7), if you're worried a child is being abused or neglected.
- **911** if a child is in immediate danger.
