## Do this now
Keep the sick person in their own room, spend as little time face-to-face as you can, and wash your hands often. Learn the emergency warning signs below.

## Steps
1. **Give the sick person their own room** if possible, and their own bathroom if you have two. Several sick people can share one sick room.
2. **Wash your hands often** with soap and water, especially after touching the sick person, their tissues, or laundry.
3. **Use a mask** when near the sick person, especially when talking face-to-face.
4. **Hold sick children with their chin on your shoulder** to avoid breathing in their germs.
5. **Get cleaner air:** open windows for fresh air when you can.
6. **Clean and disinfect surfaces** and objects that the sick person touches.
7. **Rest and fluids:** most people with flu have mild illness and get better at home. Keep drinks close; a straw or squeeze bottle helps.
8. **Stock the sick room** (from an archived 2009 CDC page): tissues, a lidded trash can lined with a bag, alcohol-based hand rub, a pitcher of drinks, a thermometer, a humidifier if you have one, and masks for the sick person to wear when they leave the room.
9. **Track medicines:** write down each dose and when it was given. Keep all medicines out of children's reach.

## If / then
- **If** an adult has trouble breathing, chest or belly pain or pressure that won't go away, ongoing dizziness or confusion, can't be woken, has a seizure, isn't peeing, or has severe weakness or muscle pain **then** get medical care right away.
- **If** a child has fast or hard breathing, ribs pulling in with each breath, bluish lips or face, chest pain, severe muscle pain (won't walk), signs of dehydration (no pee in 8 hours, dry mouth, no tears when crying), isn't alert, or has a seizure **then** get medical care right away.
- **If** a baby under 12 weeks has any fever, or a child's fever is over 104°F and doesn't come down with fever medicine **then** get medical care right away.
- **If** a fever or cough gets better and then comes back or gets worse, or a long-term health problem gets worse **then** get medical care.
- **If** the sick person is very sick or at higher risk **then** call a doctor or clinic; they may prescribe flu medicine.

## Don't
- Don't share drinking glasses, washcloths, or towels with the sick person.
- Don't touch the sick person’s face or mouth without washing your hands first.
- Don't ignore signs of serious illness or worsening symptoms.

## Get help now
- **911**: if the sick person is in immediate danger.
- **Doctor or clinic**: if the sick person is very sick or has a high risk condition.
- **Low cost care:** community health centers charge on a sliding scale (see the emergency care card).

These warning-sign lists from CDC don't cover everything. When in doubt, get checked.
