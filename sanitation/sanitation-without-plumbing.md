## Do this now
**Wash your hands after using the toilet and before eating** with soap and water. If there's no water, use **hand sanitizer with at least 60% alcohol**. Keep human waste **sealed and away from food, water, children, and pets.**

## Steps
1. **Ask before flushing.** If water or sewer lines may be broken, listen to local officials about whether toilets are safe to use. A septic system may still work if nothing is leaking or backing up.
2. **Make a bucket toilet:**
   - Use two buckets with lids if you can: one for urine, and one for poop lined with a **heavy-duty garbage bag**.
   - After each use, **cover poop with dry material** such as sawdust, shredded paper, wood chips, or leaves. It cuts down on smell and flies.
   - **Fill the bag no more than half full.**
3. **When it's time to empty:** put on gloves, **double-bag** it, tie it tightly, and store it in a separate lidded can, away from food, water, children, and pets. Follow local instructions for pickup. Don't put it in regular trash unless officials say to.
4. **Urine:** if you can, dilute it with non-drinking water and pour it on the ground or a garden, away from water sources.
5. **Set up a handwashing station:** a large water jug with a spigot, soap, and a catch bucket.
6. **Store water:** Ready.gov plans **1 gallon per person per day**, for drinking and sanitation.

## If / then
- **If** you're in a rural area and must bury waste **then** dig a hole or latrine **at least 100 feet from any well, stream, or other water source**, and not uphill of one.
- **If** your hands are visibly dirty or greasy **then** sanitizer won't work well. Use soap and the cleanest water you have.
- **If** you have an open wound **then** keep it clean and covered with a waterproof bandage, and stay out of floodwater. See a doctor if it gets red, swollen, or starts oozing.
- **If** someone has diarrhea **then** keep up the handwashing and use ORS (see the diarrhea card).

## Don't
- **Don't bathe or wash in water that may contain sewage** or chemicals, including flooded rivers and lakes.
- Don't store waste near food or drinking water.
- Don't brush your teeth with unsafe water.

## Get help now
Ask your local health department or waste hauler what to do with bagged waste. **2-1-1** for local help. See a doctor for diarrhea with signs of dehydration.
