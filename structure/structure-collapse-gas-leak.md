## Do this now
**Smell rotten eggs or sulfur, or hear hissing? Get out on foot now**, then call **911** and the gas company from well away. **Don't flip switches or start engines.**

## Steps
**Gas leak**
1. **Leave immediately on foot.** Tell everyone else to leave too. Don't look for the source. Stay upwind if you can.
2. **Don't create a spark:** no light switches (on or off), no plugging or unplugging, no matches or lighters, no starting cars, no garage-door openers, and **no phone use near the smell**.
3. **Call 911 from a neighbor's house or somewhere well away from the leak**, then call your gas company.
4. **Keep others away** and **stay out** until the gas company or responders say it's safe.

**Damaged or collapsed building**
5. **Get out and move away** from a damaged building. Don't go back in until officials say it's safe.
6. **Watch for hazards:** leaking gas, broken water lines, and **downed power lines**.

## If / then
- **If** you're trapped **then** text for help, or bang on a pipe or wall, or use a whistle instead of shouting (it saves your strength and keeps dust out). Cover your mouth with cloth.
- **If** someone else is trapped **then** call 911 and tell responders exactly where they are. Don't try to move heavy debris by yourself.
- **If** the gas was shut off **then** don't turn it back on yourself. Let the gas company do it.
- **If** you see pooled liquid, dead vegetation, a vapor cloud, or hear a roar near a pipeline **then** leave the area, don't drive into the cloud, and call 911.

## Don't
- **Don't use electrical switches, phones, flames, or vehicles** near a gas smell.
- Don't reconnect utilities yourself after a fire, flood, or quake.
- Don't enter damaged buildings to search or salvage.

## Get help now
**911** (from a safe distance), then your gas company's emergency line. Pipeline or hazmat spill: **National Response Center 1-800-424-8802**.
