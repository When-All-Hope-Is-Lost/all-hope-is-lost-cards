## Do this now
**Turn on emergency alerts on your phone.** Keep a **battery-powered NOAA Weather Radio**. After a disaster, **text instead of calling** unless it's an emergency.

## Steps
1. **Wireless Emergency Alerts (WEA)** look like texts but come with a special sound and vibration. They're **free**, **no sign-up** needed, and **not slowed down by busy networks**. If you're not getting them, check your phone settings (they may be called "Government Alerts" or "Emergency Alerts"), or ask your carrier.
2. **NOAA Weather Radio All Hazards** broadcasts National Weather Service warnings, watches, and forecasts **24/7**. It also carries non-weather emergencies, such as chemical releases or 911 outages. You need a radio that receives the weather band: **162.400 to 162.550 MHz**, seven channels.
3. **Emergency Alert System (EAS)** messages come over TV, radio, cable, and satellite, even when other systems are down.
4. **FEMA App:** get NWS alerts for up to **five locations** and find open shelters.
5. Sign up for your **local community alerts** too.

## If / then
- **If** the phone lines are jammed after a disaster **then** **send texts or use social media** to reach family. Save calls for emergencies.
- **If** you can't safely speak to 911 (an intruder, or you're deaf or speech-impaired) **then** **text 911 where available**. The rule: **call if you can, text if you can't.** Put your location first. If text isn't available where you are, you'll get a bounce-back message.
- **If** the power is out **then** use a battery-powered NOAA Weather Radio or a regular radio for EAS broadcasts.

## Don't
- Don't turn off emergency alerts on your phone.
- Don't tie up phone lines with non-emergency calls after a disaster.
- Don't rely on only one way to get warnings.

## Get help now
**911.** FEMA Helpline: **1-800-621-3362**. Text **SHELTER** and your ZIP code to **43362** to find a shelter.
