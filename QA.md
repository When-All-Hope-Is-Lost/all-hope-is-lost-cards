# QA checklist and review log

Every card must pass all six checks before it is marked `self-reviewed`. First-aid, crisis, and medication cards also need **expert review** (a clinician, EMS worker, or crisis professional) before any public release.

**Checks**
- **A** Accurate against its source: every number and instruction traced to a cited page.
- **C** Cited: sources in the front matter with URL, checked date, and license.
- **O** Actionable offline: usable with no internet; links are extras only.
- **H** No harm-to-others instructions: defensive and safety content only.
- **L** License OK: public-domain or CC BY-SA text adapted in our own words; copyrighted sources linked only; CDC disclaimer present where CDC is cited.
- **S** Self-reviewed: a re-read against the source, unsourced claims removed or marked `verify`.

## Cross-card concerns
1. **ATP 4-02.11 is combat doctrine.** Only civilian-applicable content was used; Tactical Combat Casualty Care (TCCC) phase and casualty-evacuation content was skipped. Its public-release status is verified (resolves an earlier verify flag).
2. **Bleach dose:** CDC (current) and Ready.gov (2021) differ. Cards follow CDC.
3. **Unverified reuse terms:** SAMHSA, MDC, FoodSafety.gov. Facts are cited and the wording is our own, so risk is low, but confirm before release.
4. **Wikipedia-only first-aid content** (infant choking, AED): CC BY-SA attribution is given, but medical accuracy rests on Wikipedia's summary of AHA guidance. Expert review is essential.
5. **Copyrighted sources** (Red Cross, AHA, ACS Stop the Bleed, Feeding America, NCHFP, MU Extension) appear only as links or locators.
6. **Foreign sources (cycle 3):** Met Office, Met Éireann, ECCC/CRTC, the Australian Government and BoM, and NZ Government/MetService are used for facts only. Check OGL (UK, Canada) and CC BY (Australia) terms before quoting any text.
7. **MedlinePlus encyclopedia articles are A.D.A.M.-copyrighted**, not public domain. Cards that rely on them (fa-allergic-reaction-epinephrine, and the dehydration signs in water-shortage-dehydration) need a public-domain replacement or must stay facts-only.
