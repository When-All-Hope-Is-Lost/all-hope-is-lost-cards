> **Not yet professionally reviewed.** This card has not been checked by a qualified medical professional. Please confirm with a professional.

## Do this now
Call **911** and put it on speaker, then **push hard and fast in the center of the chest**.

## Steps
1. **Check:** tap them and shout. If they're not responding and not breathing normally, **call 911** (speaker on) and follow the dispatcher.
2. **Lay them on their back** on a firm, flat surface. Kneel beside their chest.
3. **Hands:** heel of one hand on the **center of the chest (between the nipples)**, other hand on top.
4. **Push down at least 2 inches (5 cm)**, hard and smooth.
5. **Speed: 100 to 120 pushes a minute**, about two per second.
6. **Let the chest come all the way back up** between pushes, without lifting your hands off.
7. **If you're trained and willing:** give **2 rescue breaths after every 30 pushes**. With two rescuers, one can push continuously while the other gives breaths.
8. **Don't stop** until they start breathing or moving, professional help takes over, or you're too exhausted (switch with someone if you can).

## If / then
- **If** they start breathing or moving **then** stop compressions, roll them onto their side, and keep watching them.
- **If** you suspect an opioid overdose **then** give naloxone if you have it (see the overdose card), and keep CPR going.
- **If** they collapsed from choking **then** look in the mouth for the object before giving breaths; remove it only if you can see it.

## Don't
- Don't keep pausing. Compressions should be continuous until one of the stop conditions in step 8.
- Don't push too softly or too slowly.
- Don't hold back. High-quality compressions are crucial for survival (ATP 4-02.11).

## Get help now
**911** every time. CPR keeps blood moving until professionals arrive.
