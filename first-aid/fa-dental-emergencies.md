## Do this now
**Swelling of the face, fever, or trouble swallowing means go to the ER now.**

## Steps
1. Rinse with warm salt water. Floss gently to remove trapped food.
2. Use over-the-counter pain relievers as the label says.
3. Cold pack on the outside of the cheek for swelling.
4. Lost filling or broken tooth: cover with a drugstore temporary filling material; chew on the other side.
5. Knocked-out adult tooth: hold by the crown, rinse briefly, put back in the socket or keep in milk, and see a dentist within the hour if possible.
6. Keep brushing twice a day and flossing even in a crisis.
7. See a dentist as soon as you can. A cavity that reaches the nerve can cause an infection (abscess) that spreads in the body; this is rare but can cause death. It may need a filling, root canal or extraction (CDC).
8. A tooth that stops hurting still needs a dentist; waiting limits options and cavities keep growing (CDC). Low-cost care: dental and dental hygiene school clinics (CDC).

## If / then
- **If** swelling spreads to the eye, neck or under the jaw, or fever **then** go to the ER; dental infections can become deadly (see the ER card)
- **If** trouble breathing or swallowing **then** call 911

## Don't
- Don't pull teeth yourself except as a true last resort with no care available for a long time; it risks severe bleeding, infection and broken roots.
- Don't put aspirin directly on the gum; it burns.
- Don't use benzocaine or lidocaine gum gels, homeopathic teething tablets, or teething necklaces on babies and children under 2 (FDA). Rub the gums with a clean finger or give a firm, unfrozen rubber teether.

## Get help now
- 911 for breathing or swallowing trouble.
- Hospital ERs must screen and stabilize emergencies regardless of ability to pay (EMTALA); see the ER card.
