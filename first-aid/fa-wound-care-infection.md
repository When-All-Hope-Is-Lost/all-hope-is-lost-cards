## Do this now
**Stop any bleeding with direct pressure. Then gently rinse the wound with clean running or bottled water, clean around it with soap, and cover it.** **Red, swollen, oozing, or increasingly painful? Get medical care right away.**

## Steps
1. **Wash your hands** with soap and clean water if you can. Use gloves if you have them, and try not to touch the wound with your fingers.
2. **Take off rings, watches, or tight clothing** near the injury.
3. **Stop bleeding** with firm, direct pressure (see the severe bleeding card).
4. **Once bleeding stops, clean it:** look for dirt or objects, **gently flood it** with bottled or clean running water (saline is even better), and wash **around** it with soap and clean water.
5. **Pat it dry and cover it** with a bandage or a clean, dry cloth. Use a **waterproof** bandage if you might be near floodwater.
6. **Leave dirty wounds, bites, and punctures open** instead of closing them, and get them checked. Sealing a dirty wound can trap germs.
7. **Check the wound every 24 hours.**

## If / then
- **Get medical care soon if:**
  - **dirt, wood, metal, or another object is stuck in the wound**
  - it's an **animal bite** (see the rabies card)
  - it's a **puncture from a dirty object**, like stepping on a nail
  - it's **deep**
  - it has **dead tissue** (from a burn, crush, or frostbite)
- **If** it shows **signs of infection** (more pain or soreness, swelling, redness, draining, or fever) **then** get care right away.
- **If** someone with a wound becomes **confused, short of breath, has a fast heart rate, fever or shivering, extreme pain, or clammy skin** **then** these can be signs of **sepsis**. **Call 911.**
- **If** the wound is a puncture or has soil, poop, or saliva in it **then** ask a health worker whether you need a **tetanus shot**.
- **If**, days to weeks after a wound (usually about 8 days), you get **jaw cramping ("lockjaw")**, trouble swallowing, or painful muscle spasms **then** it could be **tetanus**. That's an emergency: call 911.

## Don't
- **Don't wade in floodwater, salt water, or brackish water** with an open wound. Germs there (like *Vibrio*) can cause serious infections.
- Don't close up a dirty wound, bite, or puncture.
- Don't ignore spreading redness or a fever.

## Get help now
**911** for sepsis signs or tetanus signs. A clinic, urgent care, or health center (findahealthcenter.hrsa.gov) for infected wounds, bites, punctures, and tetanus shots.
