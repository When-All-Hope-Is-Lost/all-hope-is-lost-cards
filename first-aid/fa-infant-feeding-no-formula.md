## Do this now
**Breastfeed or use donor milk if possible; never make homemade formula.**

## Steps
1. Breastfeeding is the safest emergency food: no water, power or supplies needed.
2. Feed often; frequent feeding builds supply. Stress can slow letdown but rarely stops milk.
3. A mother who recently stopped may be able to restart (relactation) with frequent nursing or pumping; get support from WIC or a lactation counselor.
4. If using formula, use ready-to-feed when water may be unsafe.
5. For powder, mix with safe (bottled or boiled and cooled) water exactly as the label says.
6. Clean and sanitize bottles, rings, caps and nipples, and check the "Use By" date (FDA), or feed by small cup if you can't clean bottles.
7. Throw out formula left in a bottle after a feeding.
8. Follow the label for storage and disposal (FDA). Never use recalled formula; if a baby ate recalled formula and has symptoms, call a health care provider right away (FDA).

## If / then
- **If** you have no formula at all **then** call 211, WIC, a hospital, or a pediatrician; ask about donor milk banks
- **If** you have concerns about a formula **then** contact the baby's health care provider and FDA at 1-888-463-6332 (1-888-INFO-FDA)
- **If** the baby has fewer wet diapers, is sleepy or floppy **then** get medical help now

## Don't
- Don't make or feed homemade formula (FDA). Recipes aren't evaluated, can lack vital nutrients, have put babies in the hospital with nutrient and electrolyte problems, and can carry kitchen bacteria that cause serious illness or death.
- Don't use cow's milk, goat's milk or plant milk in place of formula for babies under 12 months.
- Don't water down formula to make it last.
- Don't use donor milk from strangers online without screening.

## Get help now
- Sick or dehydrated baby: 911.
- 211 for local food and WIC help.
