## Do this now
**Turn the AED on and do what its voice tells you.** Keep doing CPR until it tells you to stop.

## Steps
1. **Someone calls 911** while someone else gets the AED. Public AEDs are kept in many offices, stores, schools, and transit stations.
2. **Keep doing CPR** until the AED is ready.
3. **Open it and turn it on** (many turn on when the lid opens). It will talk you through each step.
4. **Bare the chest.** Wipe it dry if it's wet. Cut or pull clothing away.
5. **Stick the pads on** where the pictures on the pads show: usually one on the upper right chest and one on the lower left side.
6. **Stand clear** when it says it's analyzing. Nobody touches the person.
7. **If it says a shock is advised,** make sure nobody is touching the person, say "Clear!", and press the shock button if it asks you to (some AEDs shock automatically).
8. **Restart CPR right away** when it tells you to, and keep following its prompts until help takes over.

## If / then
- **If** the person is a **child (about 1 to 8 years old, or under about 25 kg / 55 lb)** **then** use child pads or a child setting if there is one. If not, use adult pads. If they would touch, put one on the front of the chest and one on the back.
- **If** the person is lying in water or a puddle **then** move them to a drier spot and dry the chest first.
- **If** there's jewelry, a piercing, or an underwire bra **then** don't put a pad on metal. Move the pad, or remove the metal if it's quick.
- **If** the AED says "no shock advised" **then** keep doing CPR. It doesn't mean they're OK.

## Don't
- Don't be afraid to use it. AEDs are made for untrained people, and they won't shock unless the heart rhythm needs it.
- Don't touch the person during analysis or a shock.
- Don't stop CPR for long. Only pause when the AED tells you to.

## Get help now
**911** for anyone who collapses and isn't breathing normally. CPR plus an AED gives the best chance.
