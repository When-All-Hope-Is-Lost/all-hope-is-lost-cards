## Do this now
**Shout for help and have someone call 911. Reach or throw, don't go:** hold out a pole, branch, or rope, or throw something that floats. **Don't jump in** unless you're trained and have flotation, because a panicking person can pull you under.

## Steps
1. **Call 911**, or tell a lifeguard or a specific person to call.
2. **Reach:** lie down on your stomach on the edge so you don't get pulled in, and hold out a pole, oar, branch, towel, or rope.
3. **Throw:** if you can't reach, throw something that floats, like a life jacket, rescue tube, cooler, or ball.
4. **Once they're out of the water:** if they aren't breathing normally, **start CPR right away** (see the CPR cards). Keep going until help arrives.
5. **Keep them warm** and stay with them until responders arrive.

## If / then
- **If** you see someone **low in the water with their head tilted back, bobbing upright without moving forward, silent, or face down** **then** they may be drowning. **Drowning is often quiet**, with little splashing.
- **If** a child is missing **then** check the water first: pools, tubs, ponds, and ditches.
- **If** someone with a seizure disorder is near water **then** watch them one-on-one. A shower is safer than a bath.
- **If** water is moving fast (a flood or river) **then** don't go in. Call 911 and throw something that floats (see the flood card).

## Don't
- **Don't go into the water to rescue someone** unless you're trained and have flotation.
- **Don't drink alcohol** while swimming, boating, or watching children.
- **Don't rely on air-filled or foam toys** as life jackets, and don't hold your breath underwater for long or hyperventilate before swimming. You can black out.

## Get help now
**911.** **Prevent it:**
- Watch children **closely and constantly** near any water, bathtubs included. Put away your phone, and assign one adult per child.
- Use a **4-sided fence at least 4 feet high**, with self-closing, self-latching gates, around pools.
- Wear **life jackets** in natural water and on boats.
- Learn to swim and learn CPR.
- Drowning is the **leading cause of death for children ages 1 to 4**.
