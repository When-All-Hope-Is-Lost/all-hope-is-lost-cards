## Do this now
**Keep the injured part still** in the position you found it, and pad and splint it if you must move. Treat heavy bleeding first.

## Steps
1. **Life threats first:** stop heavy bleeding (see the bleeding card) and check breathing before worrying about the bone.
2. **Signs of a break:** bad pain with lots of swelling, a "snap" heard or felt, the limb looks a different length or shape, a crackling feeling under the skin, or loss of pulse or feeling below the injury.
3. **Check the pulse and feeling below the injury** (wrist or foot) before you splint.
4. **Splint it as you found it.** Don't straighten it. Use something rigid (board, stick, rolled cardboard or magazine) on each side, long enough to **cover the joint above and below the break**.
5. **Pad it** with clothing, a towel, or a blanket, especially at joints and the groin.
6. **Tie it** with wide strips (cloth, belts, cravats), ideally **two above and two below** the break, never on top of it.
7. **Recheck the pulse and feeling.** If fingers or toes go **numb, tingly, pale, or bluish**, or the pain gets worse, **loosen and re-tie** the splint.
8. **Arm:** support it in a sling with the **hand higher than the elbow** and the fingertips showing. With nothing to use, you can bind the arm to the chest. **Leg:** with nothing to use, tie it to the good leg.

## If / then
- **If** bone is sticking out (open fracture) **then** **don't push it back in**. Control the bleeding, cover the wound with a clean dressing, then splint.
- **If** it's probably a **sprain or strain** (twist or pull, pain, swelling, bruising, a "pop") **then** rest it, ice it, and wrap it with a compression bandage. See a provider if you can't use the joint or it isn't improving.
- **If** you suspect a **neck or back injury** (fall from height, car crash, diving) **then** don't move the person unless they're in danger. Keep the head and neck still and call 911.
- **If** the person becomes pale, sweaty, or confused **then** treat for shock, but **don't raise a broken leg** until it's splinted.

## Don't
- Don't try to straighten or "set" a broken bone.
- Don't tie with wire, string, or thin cord. They can act like a tourniquet.
- Don't tie a splint so tight that it cuts off blood flow.

## Get help now
**911** for: bone through the skin, a limb that's cold, blue, or pulseless below the injury, a suspected neck, back, hip, or thigh break, or signs of shock. Get any suspected broken bone checked by a medical provider.
