## Do this now
Call **911** (or have someone call), then press hard directly on the wound and don't let up.

## Steps
1. **Call 911**, or point at one person and tell them to call.
2. **Find the source** of the heaviest bleeding. Expose the wound if you can.
3. **Press hard and directly** on it with a cloth or gauze, using both hands and your body weight.
4. **For a deep wound,** pack it: push gauze or clean cloth into the wound toward the bleeding, then hold firm pressure for **at least 3 minutes**. Check whether blood is still seeping. If it is, add more packing and press for another 3 minutes.
5. **Wrap it tight** with a bandage over the packing to keep the pressure on. Then check that the hand or foot below it stays warm and pink with a pulse. If it turns cool, bluish, or numb, the wrap is too tight: loosen it and re-wrap.
6. **Arm or leg still bleeding heavily?** Use a **tourniquet**:
   - Put it **2 to 3 inches above the wound**, between the wound and the heart. **Never put it over a joint** (elbow, knee, wrist, ankle). If the wound is just below a joint, go above the joint.
   - **Can't tell exactly where the bleeding is coming from?** Put it **high and tight** on the limb, near the armpit or groin.
   - It can go over clothing. Pull the strap as tight as you can, then twist the rod (windlass) until **the bleeding stops**. Lock the rod in place.
7. **Note the time** the tourniquet went on and tell responders (write it on the person or on the tourniquet if you can).
8. Keep the person warm and watch for shock (see the shock card).

## If / then
- **If** you have no commercial tourniquet **then** pack and press first. Only if that fails on an arm or leg, improvise: fold a cloth to about 1½ to 2 inches wide, tie it 2 to 3 inches above the wound (not over a joint), and twist it tight with a stick (windlass), then secure the stick so it can't unwind. Improvised tourniquets often fail. Recheck them.
- **If** bleeding is from the neck, armpit, or groin **then** a limb tourniquet won't work there: pack the wound and hold hard pressure.
- **If** bleeding starts again **then** keep tightening (twisting or ratcheting) until it stops, and recheck it often.
- **If** the person gets confused, pale, or clammy **then** treat for shock and update 911.

## Don't
- **Don't remove a tourniquet** once it's on. Leave it for medical personnel.
- Don't use a tourniquet for minor bleeding, and don't leave it loose. Not tightening enough is a common error (ATP 4-02.11).
- Don't keep lifting the cloth to "check." Keep the pressure on.

## Get help now
- **911** for any heavy, spurting, or pooling bleeding, or bleeding that doesn't stop with pressure.
- Internal bleeding (after a blow to the chest or belly) can't be controlled with first aid. Get the person to emergency care fast.
