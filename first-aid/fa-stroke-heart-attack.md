## Do this now
**Call 911 right away** for sudden stroke signs or heart attack signs. **Note the time symptoms started.** Don't drive yourself.

## Steps
**Stroke: BE FAST**
1. **B, Balance:** sudden loss of balance or dizziness?
2. **E, Eyes:** sudden trouble seeing?
3. **F, Face:** ask them to smile. Does one side droop?
4. **A, Arms:** ask them to raise both arms. Does one drift down?
5. **S, Speech:** ask them to repeat a simple phrase. Is it slurred or strange?
6. **T, Time:** any of these means **call 911 now**, and **note the time** symptoms first appeared.

Other sudden stroke signs: numbness or weakness on one side, confusion, trouble understanding, or a severe headache with no known cause.

**Heart attack**
7. Watch for:
   - **Chest pain, pressure, squeezing, or fullness** lasting more than a few minutes, or that goes away and comes back.
   - **Pain in the jaw, neck, back, or one or both arms or shoulders.**
   - **Shortness of breath.**
   - **Feeling weak, light-headed, or faint**, or a cold sweat.
   - **Women** more often have unusual tiredness or nausea and vomiting.
8. **Call 911 immediately.** The sooner treatment starts, the less damage to the heart.

## If / then
- **If** the person collapses, isn't responsive, and isn't breathing normally **then** start CPR and use an AED (see the cardiac arrest and AED cards).
- **If** stroke symptoms go away after a few minutes **then** it may have been a **TIA ("mini-stroke")**, a warning sign. Get medical care right away anyway.
- **If** you're unsure whether it's serious **then** call 911 anyway. The best stroke treatments work only if given within about **3 hours** of the first symptoms.

## Don't
- **Don't drive yourself or let someone drive you.** Call 911 so treatment can start in the ambulance.
- Don't wait to see if it passes.
- Don't ignore symptoms that come and go.

## Get help now
**911.** Tell them the time symptoms started.
