## Do this now
**Call 911. Most births go fine on their own: support, don't pull.**

## Steps
1. Call 911 and stay on the line if you can.
2. Wash hands; lay out clean towels or sheets.
3. Let the mother lie or squat in the position she finds comfortable.
4. As the head comes out, support it gently. Do not pull.
5. If the cord is around the neck, gently slip it over the head.
6. Support the baby's body as it slides out. Dry the baby and place it skin to skin on the mother's chest; cover both.
7. Wipe the baby's mouth and nose. If not breathing, rub the back and soles of the feet.
8. Don't rush to cut the cord. If help is hours away, tie it tightly with clean string in two places a few inches apart and cut between with a clean blade.
9. The placenta usually comes within 30 minutes; don't pull the cord. Keep it with the baby for medical staff.
10. Rub the lower belly firmly after the placenta comes to help stop bleeding. Put the baby to the breast.

## If / then
- **If** the baby is not breathing after drying and rubbing **then** start infant CPR and get help
- **If** heavy bleeding that soaks pads quickly, or the mother faints **then** call 911, keep rubbing the belly, lay her flat with legs raised
- **If** the cord or a foot or bottom comes first **then** call 911 immediately

## Don't
- Don't pull on the baby or the cord.
- Don't let the mother or baby get cold.

## Get help now
- 911 for any birth outside a hospital.
