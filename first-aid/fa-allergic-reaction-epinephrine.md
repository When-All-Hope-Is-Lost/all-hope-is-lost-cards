## Do this now
**Use the person's epinephrine auto-injector (or nasal epinephrine) right away if they have one, and call 911.** Anaphylaxis is life-threatening and comes on fast.

## Steps
1. **Give epinephrine immediately** if it's available. Help the person use their own auto-injector (it goes **into the outer thigh muscle**) or nasal spray, following the instructions on the device. Don't delay it to make calls.
   - **If it was a food,** remove any left in their mouth or on their skin.
2. **Call 911** (or have someone call while you give the epinephrine), before calling family. **A second dose may be needed** if symptoms don't improve or get worse.
3. **Calm and reassure** them.
4. **Lay them flat and raise their feet** about 12 inches (30 cm), and cover them with a coat or blanket to help prevent shock. **Skip this** if you suspect a head, neck, back, or leg injury, if it's uncomfortable, or if they're vomiting.
5. **Watch their breathing.** If they stop breathing normally, start CPR.

## If / then
- **If** it was a **bee sting** **then** scrape the stinger off with something firm, such as a fingernail or a credit card. **Don't squeeze it with tweezers**, which pushes in more venom.
- **If** you see **signs of anaphylaxis** **then** treat it as an emergency. Signs come on fast, often within seconds or minutes:
  - trouble breathing, wheezing, or high-pitched breathing
  - swelling of the face, eyes, or tongue, or trouble swallowing
  - hives, itching, or red skin
  - a hoarse or whispered voice
  - dizziness or fainting
  - stomach pain, vomiting, or diarrhea
  - chest tightness or a racing heart
  - slurred speech
- **If** they've already had allergy shots or past mild reactions **then** don't assume they're protected.

## Don't
- **Don't rely on an asthma inhaler or antihistamine** (like diphenhydramine) instead of epinephrine. They work too slowly for anaphylaxis.
- **Don't give anything by mouth** if they're having trouble breathing.
- **Don't put a pillow under their head** if they're having trouble breathing. It can block the airway.
- Don't wait to see if the symptoms pass before calling 911.

## Get help now
**911**, or the nearest emergency room. **There's no medical reason not to use epinephrine in a severe allergic reaction.** Anyone with past serious reactions should carry epinephrine as their provider prescribes and wear a medical ID.
