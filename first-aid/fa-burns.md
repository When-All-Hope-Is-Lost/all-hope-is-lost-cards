## Do this now
**Stop the burning first:** put out flames, move the person away from the heat, chemical, or electricity, then cool the burn with clean water.

## Steps
1. **Make it safe.** Put out any flames on the person and move them away from the source.
2. **Check for worse problems first:** heavy bleeding, trouble breathing, unresponsiveness. Treat those before the burn.
3. **Cool the burn with clean, cool water** (not ice).
4. **Remove clothing and jewelry near the burn,** cutting around anything stuck to the skin. Leave stuck clothing in place.
5. **Cover loosely** with clean, dry gauze or cloth. Don't wrap tightly, since burns swell.
6. **Keep the person warm.** Burned skin loses heat fast, even on a warm day. Put something insulating under them and cover the unburned areas.
7. **Watch for shock** (see the shock card) and get medical care.

## If / then
- **If** it's an **electrical burn** **then** turn off the power first. If you can't, push or drag the person away with something dry that doesn't conduct electricity (dry wood, rope, clothing). **Never touch them or the source with bare hands.** Electrical injuries and lightning can stop breathing or the heartbeat, so be ready to do CPR.
- **If** it's a **chemical burn** **then** brush off dry chemical with a dry cloth, then flush the area with lots of running water. Don't soak the whole person in a tub. Call 911 or **Poison Help: 1-800-222-1222**.
- **If** the face is burned, or the burn happened in a smoky closed space **then** watch the breathing closely. Smoke can injure the airway. Call 911.
- **If** it's **sunburn** **then** get out of the sun, use cool cloths or a cool bath, apply moisturizing lotion, and don't break the blisters.

## Don't
- **Don't use ice** on a burn.
- Don't pull off clothing that's stuck to the burn.
- Don't put tight or elastic bandages on burns. They hurt and can cut off blood flow as it swells.

## Get help now
**911** for: large burns; burns on the face, hands, feet, groin, or major joints; burns that go through the skin (white, leathery, or charred, sometimes painless); **electrical, lightning, or chemical burns**; smoke inhalation; or burns on babies and older adults. Burns can lead to **shock and infection**.
