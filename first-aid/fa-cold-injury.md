## Do this now
Get the person **somewhere warm and dry, take off wet clothes, and wrap them in dry blankets.** If they're confused or very drowsy, **call 911**.

## Steps
1. **Get them out of the cold** into a warm room, car, or shelter.
2. **Remove wet clothing.**
3. **Hypothermia:** warm the **center of the body first** (chest, neck, head, groin) with an electric blanket if you have one, or with skin-to-skin contact under loose, dry layers.
4. **Warm drinks** help only if they're **fully awake**. No alcohol.
5. Once they're warmer, keep them dry and wrapped up, including the head and neck.
6. **Frostbite:** rewarm the area in **warm (not hot) water**, or with body heat. Afterward, cover it loosely with a clean cloth and raise it if you can.
7. **Get medical care** for any hypothermia or frostbite.

## If / then
- **If** they're **unconscious and seem to have no pulse or breathing** **then** handle them **gently**, call 911, and start CPR while you keep warming them. Some people who look dead from cold can be revived.
- **If** feet have been **cold and wet for a long time** (red, swollen, numb, tingling, itching, blisters, bad smell) **then** it may be trench foot: get to a warm, dry place, take off wet socks and boots, and dry the feet. Get medical care.
- **If** a baby has **bright red, cold skin and very low energy** **then** that's hypothermia. Warm the baby and get help.
- **If** frostbitten feet may refreeze before you reach help **then** don't thaw them yet. Walking on thawed frostbitten feet does more damage (verify with expert reviewer).

## Don't
- **Don't rub or massage** frostbitten skin.
- **Don't use a fireplace, heat lamp, radiator, stove, heating pad, or electric blanket** to warm frostbite. Numb skin burns easily.
- Don't walk on frostbitten feet or toes unless you have to. Don't give drinks to an unconscious person.

## Get help now
**Hypothermia signs:** shivering, exhaustion, confusion, fumbling hands, memory loss, slurred speech, drowsiness. **If body temperature is below 95°F, get medical help immediately.**
**Frostbite signs:** redness or pain at first, then white or grayish-yellow skin that feels firm or waxy, and numbness. It can happen even above 40°F if someone is wet and chilled. Older adults, babies in cold rooms, people outdoors for long periods, and people using alcohol or drugs are most at risk.
