## Do this now
**Stay with them, keep them safe from injury, turn them on their side, and time the seizure.** **Call 911 if it lasts more than 5 minutes.** Never put anything in their mouth.

## Steps
1. **Stay calm and stay with the person.** Keep others calm too.
2. **If they're falling, ease them to the ground.**
3. **Clear the area** of anything hard or sharp they could hit.
4. **Turn them gently onto one side** with their mouth pointing toward the ground. This keeps the airway clear.
5. **Put something soft and flat under their head**, like a folded jacket. Take off their glasses, and loosen anything tight around the neck.
6. **Time the seizure.** Most last only a few minutes.
7. **Check for a medical ID bracelet** with conditions, medicines, and emergency contacts.
8. **Afterward:** help them sit somewhere safe. Once they're alert, comfort them and explain what happened. Offer to call someone to help them get home.

## If / then
- **Call 911 if:**
  - the seizure lasts **longer than 5 minutes**
  - another seizure starts soon after
  - they have **trouble breathing or waking up** afterward
  - they're **hurt**
  - it happens **in water**
  - it's their **first seizure ever**
  - they have **diabetes and lose consciousness**
  - they're **pregnant**
- **If** someone is just staring or acting confused **then** it may still be a seizure. Not all seizures involve falling and shaking. Stay with them and keep them safe.
- **If** they aren't breathing after the seizure ends **then** call 911 and start CPR (see the cardiac arrest card).

## Don't
- **Don't hold them down** or try to stop their movements.
- **Don't put anything in their mouth.** It can break teeth or hurt the jaw.
- **Don't give mouth-to-mouth during the seizure.** People usually start breathing again on their own afterward.
- **Don't give food or water** until they're fully alert.

## Get help now
**911** for any of the reasons above. Free online seizure first aid training is available through the Epilepsy Foundation (link from the CDC page).
