## Do this now
**Confused, passing out, or very hot?** That's heat stroke. **Call 911 and cool them down fast with cold water.**

## Steps
1. **Move them somewhere cool:** shade or air conditioning.
2. **Decide how serious it is:**
   - **Heat stroke (emergency):** confusion or strange behavior, slurred speech, seizures, passing out, very high body temperature (103°F or higher), hot red skin that's dry **or** damp, and a fast, strong pulse. **Call 911.**
   - **Heat exhaustion:** heavy sweating, cold, pale, clammy skin, a fast, weak pulse, nausea or vomiting, muscle cramps, tiredness or weakness, dizziness, headache, fainting.
3. **Heat stroke: cool them fast.** Cooling comes first. Best is putting them in a tub of cold or ice water. Otherwise, soak them with a hose or shower, or cover as much skin as possible with cold, wet towels and change them often. Fan them while you mist them. Remove extra clothing.
4. **Heat exhaustion:** loosen clothing, put cool, wet cloths on the body or take a cool bath, and **sip water** (or a sports drink).
5. **Stay with them.** People with severe heat illness often don't realize how sick they are.

## If / then
- **If** heat exhaustion symptoms **get worse, last more than 1 hour, or they're vomiting** **then** get medical help right away.
- **If** it's only **heat cramps** (painful spasms during hard work or exercise) **then** stop, rest in a cool place, and drink water or a sports drink. Get help if the cramps last more than 1 hour, or if the person is on a low-sodium diet or has heart problems.
- **If** someone has drunk **a lot of plain water** and is confused, nauseated, or swollen **then** it may be low blood sodium (hyponatremia), not heat stroke. **Don't give more water.** Call 911. Small sips of a sports drink or salty snacks can help if they're awake.
- **If** it's over 90°F indoors **then** fans alone can make you hotter. Find air conditioning (call **2-1-1** for cooling centers).

## Don't
- **Don't give anything to drink** to someone with heat stroke or someone who isn't fully awake.
- Don't leave anyone, especially children or pets, in a parked car.
- Don't stop or change medicines in hot weather without talking to your doctor. Some medicines make heat illness more likely.

## Get help now
**911** for confusion, fainting, seizures, or a very high temperature. Body temperature can climb to 106°F within 10 to 15 minutes, and delay can be fatal. Check on older adults, babies, people with heart disease or asthma, pregnant people, and anyone working outside.
