## Do this now
**Watch kids for dehydration first; it is the most common danger with illness.**

## Steps
1. Give safe water often; with fever, making sure the child drinks enough is the most important thing (HealthyChildren/AAP). With diarrhea, use oral rehydration solution if you have it.
2. Keep handwashing and safe water routines strict.
3. Keep a record of vaccines and medicines.
4. Dose children's medicine by weight using the label; use the dosing cup that came with it.
5. Keep medicines and chemicals locked away.
6. Give kids routines, play and simple honest explanations.

## If / then
- **If** a baby younger than 3 months (12 weeks) has a temperature of 100.4°F (38.0°C) or higher **then** call a doctor or get care right away (HealthyChildren/AAP)
- **If** a child of any age has a temperature that rises above 104°F (40°C) repeatedly, looks very ill, is unusually drowsy or very fussy, has had a seizure, was in a very hot place such as a hot car, or has a severe headache, sore throat or ear pain, unexplained rash, or repeated vomiting or diarrhea **then** call a doctor right away (HealthyChildren/AAP)
- **If** a child with fever has immune problems (sickle cell disease, cancer, steroids) or heart problems **then** call a doctor right away (HealthyChildren/AAP)
- **If** fever lasts more than 24 hours in a child under 2, or more than 3 days (72 hours) in a child 2 or older, or the child still acts sick once the fever is down or is getting worse **then** call a doctor (HealthyChildren/AAP)
- **If** no urine for 8 hours, no tears, sunken eyes, very sleepy **then** get medical care now
- **If** trouble breathing, stiff neck, or a rash that doesn't fade when pressed **then** call 911

## Don't
- Don't give aspirin to children or teens.
- Don't give adult medicine doses to children.

## Get help now
- 911 for breathing trouble or unresponsiveness.
- Poison Help: 1-800-222-1222.
