## Do this now
If they won't wake up or are barely breathing, **give naloxone if you have it and call 911**.

## Steps
1. **Not sure if it's an overdose? Treat it like one.**
2. **Give naloxone** (Narcan or another opioid reversal medication) if you have it, then **call 911**. Say "someone is unresponsive and not breathing" and give your exact location.
3. **Try to keep them awake and breathing.**
4. **Lay them on their side** so they don't choke.
5. **No response 2 to 3 minutes after the first dose?** Give a **second dose**.
6. **Stay with them** until help arrives. Naloxone wears off in **30 to 90 minutes**, and the overdose can come back, so they still need EMS or the ER.

## If / then
- **If** they're not breathing **then** start CPR (see the CPR card) or rescue breaths, and keep going until help arrives.
- **If** someone swallowed or touched a chemical, medicine, or plant and is awake **then** call **Poison Help: 1-800-222-1222** (free, confidential, 24/7; it connects you to your local poison center).
- **If** you're worried about legal trouble for calling **then** know that most states have Good Samaritan laws that may protect the person who calls for help (check your state).
- **If** this was on purpose **then** after the emergency, use the crisis card or call or text **988**.

## Don't
- Don't wait to see if they "sleep it off."
- Don't leave them alone.
- Don't worry that naloxone will hurt them if it isn't an opioid overdose. CDC says it's safe and won't harm them, at any age.

## Get help now
Overdose signs: **can't be woken; slow or shallow breathing, or choking, gurgling, or snoring sounds; blue, gray, or purple lips or nails; tiny "pinpoint" pupils.** Call **911**. For poisonings: **1-800-222-1222**. Naloxone is sold over the counter in all 50 states, with no prescription needed (CDC).
