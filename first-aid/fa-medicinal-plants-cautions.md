## Do this now
**Treat plant remedies as weak, unpredictable medicine, never as a replacement for real treatment.**

## Steps
1. Plant strength varies a lot; there's no reliable dose.
2. Many plants interact with prescription drugs (NCCIH notes St. John's wort reduces many drugs' effects).
3. Willow bark contains salicin, related to aspirin; same cautions as aspirin, including for children.
4. Aloe gel may soothe minor burns on skin; don't swallow aloe latex.
5. Only use a plant you can identify 100%; many lethal plants look like useful ones (see the plants-not-to-eat card).
6. Write down what was taken and how much.

## If / then
- **If** someone feels ill after taking a plant **then** call Poison Help 1-800-222-1222
- **If** infection, high fever, or serious injury **then** get real medical care; herbs won't cure it

## Don't
- Don't give herbal remedies to babies, pregnant or breastfeeding people without medical advice.
- Don't stop prescribed medicines for herbs.
- Don't trust online claims that a plant cures serious disease.

## Get help now
- Poison Help: 1-800-222-1222.
- 911 for breathing trouble, seizures or unresponsiveness.
