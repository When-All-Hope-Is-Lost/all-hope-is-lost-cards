## Do this now
Call **911**, stop any bleeding first, then lay the person down and keep them warm.

## Steps
1. **Call 911.** Shock can kill if it isn't treated quickly.
2. **Stop any bleeding first** (see the severe-bleeding card). That's the most important treatment for shock.
3. **Lay them on their back with their feet raised** slightly above heart level (on a box, bag, or rolled jacket), if they can breathe comfortably lying flat. Sitting up is fine if it makes breathing easier.
4. **Keep them warm,** even in hot weather: wrap a blanket or clothing all the way around them, and put something between them and the ground.
5. **Loosen tight clothing** at the neck and waist.
6. **Shade them** from direct sun.
7. **Calm and reassure them.** Tell them help is coming and you're staying.
8. **Keep checking:** recheck bandages and tourniquets, and watch their breathing and alertness until help arrives.

## If / then
- **If** a leg is broken **then** don't raise the legs until the fracture is splinted.
- **If** they vomit, are unconscious, or you must leave them **then** turn their head to the side so they don't choke.
- **If** they become confused **and** their wrist pulse is weak or missing **then** they've likely lost a lot of blood. Tell 911 it's getting worse.

## Don't
- **Don't give food or drink.**
- Don't let them get chilled. Blood loss makes people lose heat fast.
- Don't wait for signs of shock before acting on a serious injury. Prevent it by controlling bleeding early.

## Get help now
Warning signs of shock: **confusion; weak or no wrist pulse; fast breathing; nausea; sweaty, cool, clammy skin; extreme thirst; pale or gray skin**, especially after heavy bleeding or big burns. Call **911**.
