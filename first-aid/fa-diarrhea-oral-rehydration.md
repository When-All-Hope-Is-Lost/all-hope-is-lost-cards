## Do this now
**Start drinking oral rehydration solution (ORS) right away and keep sipping.** Watery diarrhea can dehydrate someone dangerously fast, sometimes within hours.

## Steps
1. **Use ORS packets if you have them.** Mix one packet in **1 liter of boiled or treated water**, exactly as the packet says. Pharmacies sell ORS powder.
2. **No packets? Make a homemade version:** in **1 liter (about 1 quart) of clean, safe water**, dissolve **6 level teaspoons of sugar** and **½ level teaspoon of salt**. Use a real measuring spoon and stir until it all dissolves.
3. **Wash your hands, the container, and the spoon** with soap and treated water first.
4. **Sip often**, and drink more after each loose stool. As a rough daily guide, CDC suggests about **½ liter for babies and toddlers, 1 liter for children, and 3 liters for adults** who have diarrhea or vomiting.
5. **Keep breastfeeding** or giving formula to babies with diarrhea.
6. **Wash your hands** with soap after using the toilet or cleaning up, to stop it spreading (see the sanitation card).

## If / then
- **If** you have no ORS and can't make it **then** drink safe water or broth.
- **If** cholera is possible (severe watery diarrhea during an outbreak, or after unsafe water) **then** start ORS and **get medical care immediately**, drinking ORS on the way.
- **If** a child is between 6 months and 5 years old **then** ask a clinician about zinc. CDC and UNICEF/WHO recommend it for children with diarrhea.

## Don't
- **Don't use juice, soda, or sports drinks** instead of ORS. Sugary drinks can make diarrhea worse.
- **Don't add extra salt or sugar.** Too much salt is dangerous, especially for children.
- Don't make ORS with untreated water.

## Get help now
Get medical care **right away** for signs of serious dehydration (see the dehydration card), if they can't keep any fluid down, or for suspected cholera. **911** for any life-threatening emergency, such as someone who is unresponsive.
