## Do this now
**Rabbit died suddenly or has a bloody nose?** **Isolate the other rabbits and call your vet** (or the Missouri State Veterinarian, **(573) 751-3377**). Don't throw out the body until they tell you how.

## Steps
1. **Know the signs (USDA):** sudden death (usually within 1 to 3 days), a nosebleed or blood-stained nose, convulsions, fever, lethargy, not eating, trouble breathing, and loss of coordination. Some rabbits die with no warning.
2. **Isolate sick rabbits right away** and call your veterinarian. Every RHD case must be reported to state and federal animal-health officials.
3. **Ask your vet about the vaccine.** There's no cure, but USDA has authorized a vaccine to help protect healthy rabbits.
4. **Clean and disinfect** cages, feeders, and tools with a product the EPA lists as effective against RHDV2.
5. **Biosecurity every day:** keep your rabbits away from wild rabbits, isolate new rabbits before they join your group, and wash your hands and change shoes and clothes after visiting other rabbits.

## If / then
- **If** you find **several dead wild rabbits or hares** **then** don't touch them. Report them to state wildlife officials (MDC in Missouri).
- **If** you're worried about your own health **then** know that USDA says RHD **does not infect people or other animals**.
- **If** a rabbit is suffering **then** ask a veterinarian about humane euthanasia, and report the case first if RHD is possible.

## Don't
- **Don't release domestic rabbits into the wild**, ever.
- **Don't bring wild rabbits into your rabbits' space.**
- Don't dispose of a rabbit carcass without asking your vet or animal-health officials first.

## Get help now
Your vet, or the Missouri State Veterinarian / MDA Animal Health at **(573) 751-3377**. USDA: 1-866-536-7593 (the general USDA animal-disease line, also used for sick birds; verify that it takes rabbit reports).
