## Do this now
**Sudden deaths, or birds with swollen purple combs, gasping, or twisted necks? Report it today:** call the Missouri State Veterinarian (Animal Health) at **(573) 751-3377** or **USDA at 1-866-536-7593**. Keep people and other birds away.

## Steps
1. **Watch for warning signs (USDA):**
   - Sudden death or more birds dying.
   - Low energy or appetite.
   - Fewer eggs, or soft-shelled or misshapen eggs.
   - Swelling of the head, eyelids, comb, wattles, or legs, or a purple comb, wattles, or legs.
   - Gasping, runny nose, or sneezing.
   - Stumbling or a twisted neck.
   - Greenish, watery diarrhea.
2. **Report sick or dying birds right away** to your state veterinarian or USDA (numbers above). Bird flu and some other poultry diseases are **reportable**. Missouri asks for reports within 24 hours.
3. **Isolate sick birds** from the rest of the flock. Keep visitors, pets, and wild birds away.
4. **Protect yourself (CDC):** don't touch sick or dead birds, droppings, or litter with bare hands. Wear gloves, a well-fitting mask (N95 if you have one), eye protection, and boots you can clean. **Wash your hands with soap and water** afterward, and wash your clothes.
5. **Watch your own health for 10 days** after contact with a sick flock. Get care promptly for **red, irritated eyes**, fever, cough, sore throat, trouble breathing, or diarrhea, and say you were around sick birds. If you get sick, **stay home away from others** (no work or school) until you're cleared, and the health department can help arrange testing.

## If / then
- **If** a young bird (often 4 to 8 weeks or older) becomes **paralyzed in one leg or wing**, or several waste away **then** suspect **Marek's disease**, a common virus with no cure that spreads in feather dust. **Vaccination of chicks** is the only known way to prevent the tumors, so buy vaccinated chicks or ask the hatchery.
- **If** young chicks have **diarrhea (sometimes bloody)**, droop, or stop growing **then** suspect **coccidiosis**, a gut parasite spread through droppings. It's treatable: ask a vet or feed store about treatment, and consider medicated chick starter. Keep litter dry and clean.
- **If** anyone in the house gets diarrhea, fever, or cramps **then** remember that healthy-looking birds can carry ***Salmonella***. See a clinician, and tell them you keep poultry.
- **If** a bird is suffering and won't recover **then** ask a **veterinarian** about humane euthanasia. **If a reportable disease is possible, report first.** Don't kill, bury, or throw away birds until officials tell you how.

## Don't
- Don't bring new birds straight into your flock. Keep them apart first (USDA suggests about 30 days).
- Don't borrow or lend coop equipment, or wear coop shoes into the house.
- Don't let wild birds or rodents reach feed and water. Don't eat meat or eggs from sick birds.

## Get help now
Missouri State Veterinarian / MDA Animal Health: **(573) 751-3377**. USDA sick-bird line: **1-866-536-7593** (toll-free). Your own symptoms after exposure: a clinician (tell them about the birds), or **911** for trouble breathing.
