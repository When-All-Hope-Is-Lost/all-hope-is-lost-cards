## Do this now
**Keep your insulin as cool as you can, but never let it freeze.** Insulin in its original vial, pen, or cartridge can stay out of the fridge at **59–86°F for up to 28 days** and still work.

## Steps
1. **Room temperature is OK for a while:** opened or unopened insulin in the maker's vials, pens, or cartridges can stay at **59–86°F (15–30°C) for up to 28 days**. Some products differ. The once-weekly pen Awiqli allows up to 12 weeks below 86°F, so check the label.
2. **Keep it cool, out of direct sunlight, and away from heat** (not in a hot car or on a sunny windowsill).
3. **Using ice?** Don't let the insulin touch it or freeze. **Never use insulin that has frozen.**
4. **Check your blood sugar more often.** Heat makes insulin weaker over time, which can make sugar harder to control.
5. **When you can get properly stored insulin, replace** any that was exposed to extreme heat or cold.
6. **Insulin taken out of the original vial or diluted** should be thrown away within **two weeks**.

## If / then
- **If** your only insulin has been above 86°F **then** in an emergency you may still need to use it. Watch your blood sugar closely and replace it as soon as you can.
- **If** you use a pump **then** throw out insulin in the reservoir and tubing after **48 hours** (unless your pump or insulin instructions allow longer), and throw it out if it's been exposed to temperatures **above 98.6°F**.
- **If** you have to use a different brand or type of insulin **then** switch only with a doctor's guidance and close blood-sugar checks. If you can't reach a doctor, FDA's emergency switching guide explains what can be swapped (for example, one regular insulin for another, unit for unit). Talk to a pharmacist.
- **If** you're unsure whether your insulin is still good **then** call the maker's number on the package insert, or a pharmacist.

## Don't
- **Don't use frozen insulin.**
- Don't leave insulin in direct sun or a hot car.
- Don't stop taking insulin because you're worried it's warm. Check your sugar, and get replacement insulin.

## Get help now
**911** if someone with diabetes passes out or can't be woken. A pharmacist or doctor for replacement insulin (see the medication card). FDA Drug Info: **1-888-463-6332**.
