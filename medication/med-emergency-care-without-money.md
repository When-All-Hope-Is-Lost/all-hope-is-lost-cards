> **Note from the creator: why this card exists**
> My significant other had a bad tooth infection and refused to get care because of what it might cost. Friends and family begged them to go. They wouldn't. After three days of high fever, I drove them to the emergency room myself and didn't give them a choice. I honestly believe that if I hadn't, they might not be alive today. People fear bills and debt, sometimes more than death. Don't let a bill decide whether you live. Go, and if someone you love won't, take them.

## Do this now
**In an emergency, go to the emergency room or call 911. Don't let money stop you.** Under federal law (EMTALA), hospitals that take Medicare and have an emergency department must give you a medical screening exam, and stabilizing treatment if you have an emergency, **regardless of your ability to pay**.

## Steps
1. **Go.** At the ER, tell staff what's wrong. The screening exam and stabilizing care can't be refused because you can't pay or have no insurance (CMS).
2. **Ask for the financial assistance (charity care) application** before you leave, or as soon as a bill arrives. Nonprofit hospitals must have a written financial assistance policy covering emergency and other medically necessary care. They must widely publicize it: on their website, with free paper copies on request, and in public areas of the hospital (IRS).
3. **It's not too late after the bill comes.** Nonprofit hospitals must accept applications for at least **240 days** after the first bill. They must wait at least **120 days** after that bill, and give at least 30 days' notice, before steps like selling the debt or reporting it to credit agencies. They may accept applications at any time (IRS).
4. **Check the bill.** Ask for an itemized bill and question any charges you don't understand.
5. **No Surprises Act (CMS):**
   - **With insurance:** you're protected from surprise out-of-network bills for emergency room visits.
   - **Without insurance:** providers usually must give you a **good faith estimate** when you schedule care in advance or ask for one. If the bill is **at least $400 more** than the estimate, you may be able to dispute it.
6. **Tooth emergencies:**
   - **Go to the ER now** for swelling of the jaw, face, or neck with fever, or for any trouble breathing, swallowing, or opening your mouth (see the dental-infection card).
   - The ER may not provide full dental care. **Follow up with a dentist**, because an abscess usually needs a dentist to treat it.
   - **Low-cost dental care (NIDCR):** dental school and dental hygiene school clinics, where supervised students treat patients at reduced cost; and federally funded health centers, where you pay what you can afford based on income (findahealthcenter.hrsa.gov).

## If / then
- **If** you're not sure it's an emergency **then** go anyway, or call 911. The hospital decides after screening.
- **If** the hospital is a nonprofit **then** ask for its financial assistance policy and plain-language summary. They must give you a copy for free.
- **If** you get an estimate as an uninsured patient and the bill is much higher **then** look at the dispute process on cms.gov/medical-bill-rights.
- **If** a debt collector calls about a hospital bill **then** ask whether you were offered financial assistance, and apply if you're still in the application window.

## Don't
- Don't skip the ER for a real emergency because of cost.
- Don't ignore hospital bills. Apply for financial assistance instead.

> **Note from the creator**
> Some people think giving a fake name or no contact information at the ER is a way to avoid the bill. It isn't a good idea:
> - **It may be illegal.** Depending on your state, it can count as fraud or theft of services.
> - **It can hurt your health.** Care under a wrong identity can mix up your medical records, allergies, test results, and follow-up calls, so you could miss results or get the wrong treatment later.
> - **There's a legal way to cut the bill.** Financial assistance (charity care) can reduce or erase a hospital bill. Give your real information and ask for the application.

## Get help now
**911** for emergencies. **2-1-1** for local help with medical bills and low-cost clinics. CMS No Surprises help desk: cms.gov/medical-bill-rights. Health centers: findahealthcenter.hrsa.gov.
