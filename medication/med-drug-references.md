## Do this now
**For the official facts about a medicine, read its FDA label on DailyMed** (dailymed.nlm.nih.gov) or the paper insert that came with it. Ask a pharmacist if anything is unclear.

## Steps
1. **DailyMed (NIH National Library of Medicine):** the official FDA-approved labels (package inserts) for prescription and over-the-counter drugs. US Government, public domain. Search by brand or generic name. Check the **Boxed Warning**, **Dosage and Administration**, **Warnings**, **Drug Interactions**, and **How Supplied/Storage** sections.
2. **MedlinePlus Drug Information** (medlineplus.gov/druginformation.html): plain-language drug pages. These are copyrighted (American Society of Health-System Pharmacists), so they're linked, not copied into this app.
3. **FDA Drugs@FDA** (accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cder/daf/): approval history and labels. The **Orange Book** lists approved drugs and which generics are rated equivalent, which helps when you ask about a cheaper generic.
4. **Copyrighted references (further reading only, not bundled):** *Meyler's Side Effects of Drugs*, the *Physician's Desk Reference (PDR)*, and Drugs.com.
5. **Offline option for app builders (future):** DailyMed publishes its full label set for download. On 2026-10-17 the official zip files totaled roughly **57 GB** for all sets, about **17 GB** for human prescription labels alone (estimate from the listed file sizes; it changes often). That is too large to bundle today; a trimmed subset is a possible future option.

## If / then
- **If** a label and a website disagree **then** trust the current FDA label and ask a pharmacist.
- **If** you think someone took the wrong or too much medicine **then** call **Poison Help: 1-800-222-1222**, or **911** if they're hard to wake or not breathing well.

## Don't
- Don't change a dose based on a website. Ask the prescriber or pharmacist.
- Don't rely on old printouts: labels get updated.

## Get help now
**911** for emergencies. **Poison Help: 1-800-222-1222**. FDA drug info: **1-888-463-6332**.
