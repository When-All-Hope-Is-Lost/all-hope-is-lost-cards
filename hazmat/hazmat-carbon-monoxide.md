## Do this now
**Get everyone (and pets) outside into fresh air right now, then call 911.** Don't stay inside to look for the source.

## Steps
1. **Get out.** Bring everyone out, including pets. Help anyone who's drowsy or confused.
2. **Call 911** from outside. For questions when no one has symptoms, call **Poison Help: 1-800-222-1222**.
3. **Stay out** until emergency responders say it's safe to go back in, even if you start to feel better.
4. **Get checked.** Anyone with symptoms needs medical care. CO poisoning can kill and can cause lasting harm.
5. **Afterward,** have the furnace, water heater, and other gas-, oil-, or coal-burning appliances serviced by a qualified technician. CDC advises doing this every year.

## If / then
- **If** someone is unresponsive and not breathing **then** get them into fresh air (only if you can do it quickly without going back into heavy fumes), call 911, and start CPR.
- **If** several people (or pets) feel sick at once, or feel better outdoors **then** suspect CO even if no alarm sounded.
- **If** the power is out **then** run generators **only outdoors, more than 20 feet from windows, doors, and vents**, and never in a home or garage, even with doors open.
- **If** you're stuck in a car in snow **then** clear snow from the exhaust pipe, run the engine only about 10 minutes an hour, and crack a window.

## Don't
- Don't use a generator, charcoal grill, camp stove, or gasoline engine inside a home, basement, garage, tent, or car.
- Don't run a car or truck in a garage attached to a house, even with the door open.
- Don't ignore a CO alarm. Get to fresh air first, ask questions later.

## Get help now
CO has **no color or smell**. Symptoms are **"flu-like": headache, dizziness, weakness, upset stomach, vomiting, chest pain, confusion**. Sleeping or drunk people can die before they notice symptoms. Call **911**.
**Prevent it:** put battery-operated or battery-backup CO alarms **near every sleeping area**. Check the batteries each spring and fall when the clocks change, and replace the alarms as the maker says (CDC says about every 5 years).
