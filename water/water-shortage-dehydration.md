## Do this now
Drink what you need today. **Don't ration drinking water** unless authorities tell you to. Then work on finding more for tomorrow.

## Steps
1. **Plan on at least 1 gallon per person per day** for drinking and sanitation. A normally active person needs about ¾ gallon of fluids a day.
2. **Children, nursing mothers, sick people, and anyone in hot weather need more.** In very hot temperatures, water needs can double.
3. **Use your body's water wisely:** cut back on activity and stay cool to need less.
4. **Drink water you know is safe first.** Save questionable water (cloudy tap water, streams, ponds) for treatment (see the water-treatment card).
5. **Find more:** ask 211, local officials, food banks, and shelters about water distribution.
6. **Storing water:** use food-grade containers. Clean them with dish soap, sanitize with **1 teaspoon of unscented bleach per quart of water**, then fill. Replace stored (non-commercial) water **every 6 months**. Keep sealed commercial bottled water in a cool, dark place.

## If / then
- **If** you can't treat questionable water **then** put off drinking it as long as possible, **but don't become dehydrated**.
- **If** it's hot **then** expect to need much more water, and stay in shade or cool places.
- **If** someone is sick, very young, old, or nursing **then** give them priority.

## Don't
- Don't drink caffeinated or carbonated drinks, or alcohol, *instead of* water. Caffeine and alcohol dehydrate you.
- Don't ration below what you need today unless authorities order it.
- Don't store water in containers that held chemicals.

## Get help now
**Signs of dehydration:** in adults, strong thirst, dry mouth, peeing and sweating less, dark urine, dry skin, tiredness, and dizziness. In babies and young children: dry mouth and tongue, crying without tears, **no wet diaper for 3 hours or more**, high fever, unusual sleepiness, irritability, and sunken eyes.
**Get medical help right away** if there's also **confusion, fainting, no urine, a fast heartbeat, fast breathing, or signs of shock**. Older adults (who may feel less thirst) and infants are at higher risk. For mild dehydration, drink water. Oral rehydration solution (sold without a prescription) helps children, and sports drinks help replace lost electrolytes. Call **911** for collapse or confusion.
