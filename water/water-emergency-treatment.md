## Do this now
**Boil clear water for 1 minute** (3 minutes above 6,500 feet), and let it cool. Boiling is the best method.

## Steps
1. **Clear it first:** let dirt settle, or strain it through coffee filters or layers of clean cloth.
2. **Boil (best):** bring it to a **rolling boil for 1 minute** (**3 minutes** at elevations above 6,500 feet). Let it cool. Pouring it back and forth between two clean containers improves the taste (Ready.gov).
3. **Can't boil? Use bleach.** Use only **unscented** household liquid bleach (check the label: **5% to 9% sodium hypochlorite**). Don't use scented, color-safe, or added-cleaner bleach.
   - **1 quart or liter:** 2 drops
   - **1 gallon:** **8 drops** (about ½ mL, or a little less than ⅛ teaspoon)
   - **5 gallons:** 40 drops (2½ mL, or ½ teaspoon)
   - **Cloudy, murky, colored, or very cold water: double it** (e.g., 16 drops per gallon).
4. **Stir and wait at least 30 minutes** before drinking.
5. **Store it** in clean, sanitized containers with tight covers.

## If / then
- **If** you only have a portable filter **then** know that most don't remove bacteria or viruses. Use one with a pore size of 1 micron or smaller for parasites (0.3 micron or smaller for bacteria), then disinfect the water afterward to kill viruses.
- **If** officials issue a **boil-water advisory** **then** boil tap water. Don't rely on a typical household or portable filter, since most don't remove bacteria or viruses.
- **If** the water may contain **chemicals, fuel, or toxins** **then** boiling, bleach, and filters won't make it safe. Find another source.
- **If** you're using bleach or tablets **then** know they work less well against the parasites *Cryptosporidium* and *Giardia*. Boil when you can.

## Don't
- Don't drink water you suspect is contaminated without treating it, unless there's truly nothing else and you're getting dehydrated.
- Don't use pool chemicals or bleach with additives.
- Don't forget: use treated water for brushing teeth, washing food, and making ice too.

## Get help now
Diarrhea with signs of dehydration, bloody stool, or high fever after drinking untreated water: get medical care. Follow local officials' water notices.
