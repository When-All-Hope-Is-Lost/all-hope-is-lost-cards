## Do this now
**Water and warmth first, food second. Before you hunt, fish, trap or forage, check the rules: South Carolina Department of Natural Resources, https://www.dnr.sc.gov/hunting.html**

## Steps
1. **Water.** Treat all surface water (boil 1 minute; see water-emergency-treatment). Known risks here:
- Storm surge can contaminate wells and public systems; follow boil-water notices.
- Floods contaminate wells and springs; disinfect and test after flooding.
- Arsenic: USGS estimates roughly 28,000 people here use private wells likely above EPA's arsenic limit (2017 model estimate). Boiling does NOT remove arsenic; test private wells.
2. **Heat and shelter.** Climate class: hot-humid. Heat is the main threat. Plan shade, airflow and water; rest midday; know cooling centers; watch for heat illness.
3. **Fish first, then small game.** Fishing usually needs the least gear and energy. Commonly caught here:
- bluegill and other bream (seasons: check agency)
- crappie (seasons: check agency)
- largemouth bass (seasons: check agency)
4. **Small game** commonly hunted here (license and season required):
- rabbit (seasons: check agency)
- gray squirrel (seasons: check agency)
- fox squirrel (seasons: check agency)
- white-tailed deer (not yet confirmed with the agency)
- wild turkey (not yet confirmed with the agency)
- Feral hogs occur in parts of this state, but rules differ widely; some states ban hunting or trapping them to support eradication. Check the agency before taking any hog.
5. **Forage only what you can identify with certainty.** Edible plants found here, with native status and lookalike warnings (introduced plants listed last):
- **blackberry/raspberry** (*Rubus spp.*), native species occur here; some types or populations are introduced (USDA PLANTS): No dangerous lookalikes, but confirm it is a thorny bramble and eat only ripe fruit.
- **dandelion** (*Taraxacum officinale*), native species occur here; some types or populations are introduced (USDA PLANTS): Edible, but avoid sprayed lawns, roadsides and industrial sites.
- **persimmon** (*Diospyros virginiana*), native here (USDA PLANTS): Unripe fruit is not toxic but very astringent; eat only soft, ripe fruit.
- **pawpaw** (*Asimina triloba*), native here (USDA PLANTS): Eat only the ripe pulp. Skin and seeds can cause vomiting.
- **mulberry** (*Morus spp.*), native species occur here; some types or populations are introduced (USDA PLANTS): Unripe berries upset the stomach. *(warning not yet checked against an extension source)*
- **wild grape** (*Vitis spp.*), native species occur here; some types or populations are introduced (USDA PLANTS): Toxic lookalike: Canada moonseed. Each moonseed berry has one flat crescent-shaped seed; never eat it.
- **wild onion/garlic** (*Allium spp.*), native species occur here; some types or populations are introduced (USDA PLANTS): Toxic lookalike: death camas, which has no onion smell in any part. No onion smell = do not eat.
6. **Growing food:** USDA 2023 hardiness zones across this state's ZIP codes: 8a-9b. Look up your ZIP at planthardiness.ars.usda.gov. For local frost dates and planting calendars ask Clemson Cooperative Extension (https://www.clemson.edu/extension/). General schedules, garden size per adult, and canning: see food-garden-planting-calendar, food-garden-layout-by-size and food-canning-basics.
7. **Preserve and store** game safely: see food-smoking-curing-meat and food-storing-without-power.

## If / then
- **If** you are unsure of a plant, mushroom or berry **then** don't eat it; call Poison Help 1-800-222-1222 if someone did.
- **If** the water is scummy, discolored, or near mines, farms or floodwater **then** find another source; boiling does not remove chemicals or algal toxins.
- **If** a hazard hits (common here: weather-hurricane, weather-flood, weather-tornado, weather-earthquake, weather-extreme-heat) **then** open that weather card.

## Don't
- Don't hunt, fish or trap without the right license, season and method. Survival needs do not override the law.
- Don't eat wild mushrooms without in-person expert confirmation.
- Don't use grills, generators or fuel heaters indoors (carbon monoxide).

## Get help now
Emergency: **911**. Poison Help: **1-800-222-1222**. Food, shelter and utility help: **211**. Rules and licenses: https://www.dnr.sc.gov/
