## Do this now
**Water and warmth first, food second. Before you hunt, fish, trap or forage, check the rules: Texas Parks and Wildlife Department, https://tpwd.texas.gov/regulations/outdoor-annual/**

## Steps
1. **Water.** Treat all surface water (boil 1 minute; see water-emergency-treatment). Known risks here:
- Drought: supplies can be rationed; store water early.
- Flash floods in dry washes; never camp or drive in them.
- Storm surge can contaminate wells and public systems; follow boil-water notices.
- Arsenic: USGS estimates roughly 95,000 people here use private wells likely above EPA's arsenic limit (2017 model estimate). Boiling does NOT remove arsenic; test private wells.
2. **Heat and shelter.** Climate class: hot-mixed. Heat is the main threat. Plan shade, airflow and water; rest midday; know cooling centers; watch for heat illness.
3. **Fish first, then small game.** Fishing usually needs the least gear and energy. Commonly caught here:
- channel catfish (seasons: check agency)
- bluegill and sunfish (seasons: check agency)
- largemouth bass (seasons: check agency)
4. **Small game** commonly hunted here (license and season required):
- rabbits and hares (seasons: check agency)
- squirrel (seasons: check agency)
- mourning dove (seasons: check agency)
- white-tailed deer (not yet confirmed with the agency)
- Feral hogs occur in parts of this state, but rules differ widely; some states ban hunting or trapping them to support eradication. Check the agency before taking any hog.
5. **Forage only what you can identify with certainty.** Edible plants found here, with native status and lookalike warnings (introduced plants listed last):
- **prickly pear** (*Opuntia spp.*), native species occur here; some types or populations are introduced (USDA PLANTS): Use gloves and tongs. Remove spines and tiny glochids (singe, scrub or peel) before eating.
- **mulberry** (*Morus spp.*), native species occur here; some types or populations are introduced (USDA PLANTS): Unripe berries upset the stomach. *(warning not yet checked against an extension source)*
- **dandelion** (*Taraxacum officinale*), native species occur here; some types or populations are introduced (USDA PLANTS): Edible, but avoid sprayed lawns, roadsides and industrial sites.
- **purslane** (*Portulaca oleracea*), native species occur here; some types or populations are introduced (USDA PLANTS): Toxic lookalike: prostrate spurge, which has milky white sap. Purslane has clear sap and yellow flowers.
- **persimmon** (*Diospyros virginiana*), native here (USDA PLANTS): Unripe fruit is not toxic but very astringent; eat only soft, ripe fruit.
- **acorns (leached)** (*Quercus spp.*), native species occur here; some types or populations are introduced (USDA PLANTS): Grind and soak in cold water, changing it often, until no longer bitter (removes tannins). Don't confuse with toxic buckeye nuts, which have no scaly cap.
6. **Growing food:** USDA 2023 hardiness zones across this state's ZIP codes: 6b-10b. Look up your ZIP at planthardiness.ars.usda.gov. For local frost dates and planting calendars ask Texas A&M AgriLife Extension (https://agrilifeextension.tamu.edu/). General schedules, garden size per adult, and canning: see food-garden-planting-calendar, food-garden-layout-by-size and food-canning-basics.
7. **Preserve and store** game safely: see food-smoking-curing-meat and food-storing-without-power.

## If / then
- **If** you are unsure of a plant, mushroom or berry **then** don't eat it; call Poison Help 1-800-222-1222 if someone did.
- **If** the water is scummy, discolored, or near mines, farms or floodwater **then** find another source; boiling does not remove chemicals or algal toxins.
- **If** a hazard hits (common here: weather-extreme-heat, weather-hurricane, weather-tornado, weather-flood, weather-drought, weather-winter-storm) **then** open that weather card.

## Don't
- Don't hunt, fish or trap without the right license, season and method. Survival needs do not override the law.
- Don't eat wild mushrooms without in-person expert confirmation.
- Don't use grills, generators or fuel heaters indoors (carbon monoxide).

## Get help now
Emergency: **911**. Poison Help: **1-800-222-1222**. Food, shelter and utility help: **211**. Rules and licenses: https://tpwd.texas.gov/ (Large state: the Panhandle, Hill Country and Gulf coast differ a lot.)
