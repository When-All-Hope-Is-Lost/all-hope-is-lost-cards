## Do this now
**Water and warmth first, food second. Before you hunt, fish, trap or forage, check the rules: Alaska Department of Fish and Game, https://www.adfg.alaska.gov/index.cfm?adfg=regulations.main**

## Steps
1. **Water.** Treat all surface water (boil 1 minute; see water-emergency-treatment). Known risks here:
- Clear mountain streams can carry Giardia; treat all surface water.
- Pipes and supplies freeze; keep water from freezing.
- Many communities are off the road system; resupply may take weeks.
2. **Heat and shelter.** Climate class: subarctic-arctic. Cold winters are the main threat. Plan to heat one small room; insulate windows; layer wool or synthetics; never use grills, ovens or generators indoors.
3. **Fish first, then small game.** Fishing usually needs the least gear and energy. Commonly caught here:
- salmon (Chinook, chum, coho, pink, sockeye) (seasons: check agency)
- Arctic grayling (seasons: check agency)
- Dolly Varden (seasons: check agency)
- rainbow trout (seasons: check agency)
- northern pike (seasons: check agency)
4. **Small game** commonly hunted here (license and season required):
- snowshoe hare (seasons: check agency)
- spruce grouse (seasons: check agency)
- moose (licensed) (seasons: check agency)
- caribou (licensed) (seasons: check agency)
- willow and rock ptarmigan (all ptarmigan rules vary by unit) (seasons: check agency)
5. **Forage only what you can identify with certainty.** Edible plants found here, with native status and lookalike warnings (introduced plants listed last):
- **blueberry/huckleberry** (*Vaccinium spp.*), native here (USDA PLANTS): Toxic lookalikes include baneberry (highly toxic red or white berries) and nightshade; know the plant, not just the berry.
- **lingonberry/cloudberry** (*Vaccinium vitis-idaea / Rubus chamaemorus*), native here (USDA PLANTS): Toxic lookalike: baneberry (highly toxic red or white berries).
- **fireweed** (*Chamerion angustifolium*), native here (USDA PLANTS): Confirm ID; young shoots only. *(warning not yet checked against an extension source)*
- **rose hips** (*Rosa spp.*), native here (USDA PLANTS): Remove the seeds and hairy inner lining; the hairs irritate the throat and gut.
6. **Growing food:** USDA 2023 hardiness zones across this state's ZIP codes: 1a-8b. Look up your ZIP at planthardiness.ars.usda.gov. For local frost dates and planting calendars ask University of Alaska Fairbanks Cooperative Extension (https://www.uaf.edu/ces/). General schedules, garden size per adult, and canning: see food-garden-planting-calendar, food-garden-layout-by-size and food-canning-basics.
7. **Preserve and store** game safely: see food-smoking-curing-meat and food-storing-without-power.

## If / then
- **If** you are unsure of a plant, mushroom or berry **then** don't eat it; call Poison Help 1-800-222-1222 if someone did.
- **If** the water is scummy, discolored, or near mines, farms or floodwater **then** find another source; boiling does not remove chemicals or algal toxins.
- **If** a hazard hits (common here: weather-earthquake, weather-tsunami, weather-winter-storm, weather-wildfire-smoke, weather-flood) **then** open that weather card.

## Don't
- Don't hunt, fish or trap without the right license, season and method. Survival needs do not override the law.
- Don't eat wild mushrooms without in-person expert confirmation.
- Don't use grills, generators or fuel heaters indoors (carbon monoxide).

## Get help now
Emergency: **911**. Poison Help: **1-800-222-1222**. Food, shelter and utility help: **211**. Rules and licenses: https://www.adfg.alaska.gov/ (Subsistence rules exist but have their own eligibility and permits; check ADF&G.)
