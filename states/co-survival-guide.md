## Do this now
**Water and warmth first, food second. Before you hunt, fish, trap or forage, check the rules: Colorado Parks and Wildlife, https://cpw.state.co.us/hunting**

## Steps
1. **Water.** Treat all surface water (boil 1 minute; see water-emergency-treatment). Known risks here:
- Clear mountain streams can carry Giardia; treat all surface water.
- Drought: supplies can be rationed; store water early.
- Old mine drainage can contaminate streams with metals; avoid orange or unusual streams.
- Arsenic: USGS estimates roughly 14,000 people here use private wells likely above EPA's arsenic limit (2017 model estimate). Boiling does NOT remove arsenic; test private wells.
2. **Heat and shelter.** Climate class: cold-dry. Cold winters are the main threat. Plan to heat one small room; insulate windows; layer wool or synthetics; never use grills, ovens or generators indoors.
3. **Fish first, then small game.** Fishing usually needs the least gear and energy. Commonly caught here:
- rainbow trout (seasons: check agency)
- cutthroat trout (catch-and-release on many waters) (seasons: check agency)
4. **Small game** commonly hunted here (license and season required):
- cottontail rabbit (seasons: check agency)
- snowshoe hare (seasons: check agency)
- dusky grouse (west of I-25) (seasons: check agency)
- mule deer (not yet confirmed with the agency)
- elk (usually limited licenses) (not yet confirmed with the agency)
5. **Forage only what you can identify with certainty.** Edible plants found here, with native status and lookalike warnings (introduced plants listed last):
- **blueberry/huckleberry** (*Vaccinium spp.*), native here (USDA PLANTS): Toxic lookalikes include baneberry (highly toxic red or white berries) and nightshade; know the plant, not just the berry.
- **rose hips** (*Rosa spp.*), native species occur here; some types or populations are introduced (USDA PLANTS): Remove the seeds and hairy inner lining; the hairs irritate the throat and gut.
- **dandelion** (*Taraxacum officinale*), native species occur here; some types or populations are introduced (USDA PLANTS): Edible, but avoid sprayed lawns, roadsides and industrial sites.
- **pinyon pine nuts** (*Pinus edulis / P. monophylla*), native here (USDA PLANTS): Some other conifers (yew) are toxic; know the tree. *(warning not yet checked against an extension source)*
- **wild onion/garlic** (*Allium spp.*), native species occur here; some types or populations are introduced (USDA PLANTS): Toxic lookalike: death camas, which has no onion smell in any part. No onion smell = do not eat.
6. **Growing food:** USDA 2023 hardiness zones across this state's ZIP codes: 4a-7a. Look up your ZIP at planthardiness.ars.usda.gov. For local frost dates and planting calendars ask Colorado State University Extension (https://extension.colostate.edu/). General schedules, garden size per adult, and canning: see food-garden-planting-calendar, food-garden-layout-by-size and food-canning-basics.
7. **Preserve and store** game safely: see food-smoking-curing-meat and food-storing-without-power.

## If / then
- **If** you are unsure of a plant, mushroom or berry **then** don't eat it; call Poison Help 1-800-222-1222 if someone did.
- **If** the water is scummy, discolored, or near mines, farms or floodwater **then** find another source; boiling does not remove chemicals or algal toxins.
- **If** a hazard hits (common here: weather-winter-storm, weather-wildfire-smoke, weather-flood, weather-drought, weather-thunderstorm-lightning) **then** open that weather card.

## Don't
- Don't hunt, fish or trap without the right license, season and method. Survival needs do not override the law.
- Don't eat wild mushrooms without in-person expert confirmation.
- Don't use grills, generators or fuel heaters indoors (carbon monoxide).

## Get help now
Emergency: **911**. Poison Help: **1-800-222-1222**. Food, shelter and utility help: **211**. Rules and licenses: https://cpw.state.co.us/ (High altitude makes cold and dehydration worse.)
