## Do this now
**Water and warmth first, food second. Before you hunt, fish, trap or forage, check the rules: MassWildlife (Division of Fisheries and Wildlife), https://www.mass.gov/hunting-regulations**

## Steps
1. **Water.** Treat all surface water (boil 1 minute; see water-emergency-treatment). Known risks here:
- Many homes use private wells; test them.
- Shellfish can carry biotoxins that cooking does not destroy; check state closures.
- Arsenic: USGS estimates roughly 31,000 people here use private wells likely above EPA's arsenic limit (2017 model estimate). Boiling does NOT remove arsenic; test private wells.
2. **Heat and shelter.** Climate class: cold-humid. Cold winters are the main threat. Plan to heat one small room; insulate windows; layer wool or synthetics; never use grills, ovens or generators indoors.
3. **Fish first, then small game.** Fishing usually needs the least gear and energy. Commonly caught here:
- bluegill and other sunfish (seasons: check agency)
- yellow perch (seasons: check agency)
- largemouth bass (seasons: check agency)
- brook trout (seasons: check agency)
- chain pickerel (seasons: check agency)
4. **Small game** commonly hunted here (license and season required):
- cottontail rabbit (seasons: check agency)
- snowshoe hare (seasons: check agency)
- gray squirrel (seasons: check agency)
- ruffed grouse (seasons: check agency)
- white-tailed deer (seasons: check agency)
- wild turkey (seasons: check agency)
5. **Forage only what you can identify with certainty.** Edible plants found here, with native status and lookalike warnings (introduced plants listed last):
- **blackberry/raspberry** (*Rubus spp.*), native species occur here; some types or populations are introduced (USDA PLANTS): No dangerous lookalikes, but confirm it is a thorny bramble and eat only ripe fruit.
- **dandelion** (*Taraxacum officinale*), native species occur here; some types or populations are introduced (USDA PLANTS): Edible, but avoid sprayed lawns, roadsides and industrial sites.
- **black walnut / hickory nuts** (*Juglans nigra / Carya spp.*), native here (USDA PLANTS): Buckeye nuts look similar and are toxic. *(warning not yet checked against an extension source)*
- **wild grape** (*Vitis spp.*), native species occur here; some types or populations are introduced (USDA PLANTS): Toxic lookalike: Canada moonseed. Each moonseed berry has one flat crescent-shaped seed; never eat it.
- **blueberry/huckleberry** (*Vaccinium spp.*), native here (USDA PLANTS): Toxic lookalikes include baneberry (highly toxic red or white berries) and nightshade; know the plant, not just the berry.
- **rose hips** (*Rosa spp.*), native species occur here; some types or populations are introduced (USDA PLANTS): Remove the seeds and hairy inner lining; the hairs irritate the throat and gut.
- **cattail** (*Typha spp.*), native species occur here; some types or populations are introduced (USDA PLANTS): Young shoots resemble toxic irises; only harvest from clean water. *(warning not yet checked against an extension source)*
6. **Growing food:** USDA 2023 hardiness zones across this state's ZIP codes: 5b-7b. Look up your ZIP at planthardiness.ars.usda.gov. For local frost dates and planting calendars ask UMass Extension (https://ag.umass.edu/). General schedules, garden size per adult, and canning: see food-garden-planting-calendar, food-garden-layout-by-size and food-canning-basics.
7. **Preserve and store** game safely: see food-smoking-curing-meat and food-storing-without-power.

## If / then
- **If** you are unsure of a plant, mushroom or berry **then** don't eat it; call Poison Help 1-800-222-1222 if someone did.
- **If** the water is scummy, discolored, or near mines, farms or floodwater **then** find another source; boiling does not remove chemicals or algal toxins.
- **If** a hazard hits (common here: weather-winter-storm, weather-hurricane, weather-flood) **then** open that weather card.

## Don't
- Don't hunt, fish or trap without the right license, season and method. Survival needs do not override the law.
- Don't eat wild mushrooms without in-person expert confirmation.
- Don't use grills, generators or fuel heaters indoors (carbon monoxide).

## Get help now
Emergency: **911**. Poison Help: **1-800-222-1222**. Food, shelter and utility help: **211**. Rules and licenses: https://www.mass.gov/orgs/division-of-fisheries-and-wildlife
