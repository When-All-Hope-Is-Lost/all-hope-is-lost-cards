## Do this now
**Keep the fridge and freezer closed.** Use generators **only outdoors**, at least 20 feet from windows, doors, and garages. **Never heat with a gas stove or oven.**

## Steps
1. **Keep refrigerator and freezer doors shut.** A fridge keeps food cold for **about 4 hours**, and a **full freezer** holds its temperature for **about 48 hours**. Use coolers with ice if you need to.
2. **Unplug appliances and electronics.** Power can come back with surges that damage them.
3. **Generators, camp stoves, and charcoal grills go outdoors only**, at least **20 feet** from windows, doors, and attached garages. Use heavy-duty extension cords, and let a generator cool before refueling it.
4. **Use flashlights** (ideally one for each person) and power banks to charge phones.
5. **Extreme heat or cold?** Go to a community location with power. Ask local officials or call 2-1-1 about warming and cooling centers.
6. **Medical devices or refrigerated medicine:** use your backup plan, and call your provider for guidance.

## If / then
- **If** food has been **at 40°F or warmer for 2 hours or more**, or smells, looks, or feels odd **then** **throw it out. When in doubt, throw it out.**
- **If** the power has been out **more than a day** **then** throw away medicine that should be refrigerated unless its label says otherwise, and call your doctor or pharmacist right away for a new supply.
- **If** anyone gets a headache, dizziness, or nausea while a generator or heater is running **then** get everyone outside and call 911 (see the carbon monoxide card).

## Don't
- **Don't use a generator, grill, or camp stove indoors** or in a garage.
- **Don't heat your home with a gas stovetop or oven.**
- Don't touch a wet generator.

## Get help now
**911** for carbon monoxide symptoms, or a medical device emergency. **2-1-1** for warming and cooling centers. Report the outage to your electric company.
