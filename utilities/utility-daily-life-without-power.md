## Do this now
**Cook, heat and run generators outdoors only. Carbon monoxide kills people in long outages.**

## Steps
1. **Light:** flashlights and battery lanterns, not candles, where you can.
2. **Cooking:** camp stoves and grills outdoors only, at least 20 feet from doors and windows (see utility-power-outage, hazmat-carbon-monoxide).
3. **Warmth or cooling:** gather everyone into one room, use layers and blankets in cold, and shade and airflow in heat. Go to a warming or cooling center if it gets dangerous (211).
4. **Water:** store and treat water (water-emergency-treatment). Ration sweat, not water.
5. **Sanitation:** set up a toilet plan before you need one (sanitation-without-plumbing) and keep hands clean (inf-handwashing-no-water).
6. **Food:** eat perishables first (food-storing-without-power).
7. **Phones and news:** charge from car or battery banks; keep a battery or hand-crank radio for alerts (comms-emergency-alerts).
8. **Routine and morale:** keep regular sleep, meals, chores and daylight activity; check on neighbors (mh-coping-with-stress).
9. **Medical devices and refrigerated medicine:** follow your backup plan (med-insulin-without-power).

## If / then
- **If** anyone gets headache, dizziness or nausea indoors with a fuel-burning device on **then** get everyone outside into fresh air and call 911.
- **If** the outage will outlast your water, heat or medicine **then** move to a shelter or another place with power before it becomes an emergency.

## Don't
- Don't use ovens, grills, generators or fuel heaters inside, even with a window open.
- Don't connect a generator to house wiring without a proper transfer switch.

## Get help now
**911** for carbon monoxide symptoms, fire or medical emergencies. **211** for shelters, warming and cooling centers.
