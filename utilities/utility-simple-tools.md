## Do this now
**Improve what you already have before you make something new. A cut from a tool can become an infection fast.**

## Steps
1. **Inventory first:** knives, saws, pliers, tape, wire, rope, tarps, containers and fasteners are worth more than anything you can make.
2. **Cordage and lashing:** ATP 3-50.21 covers lashings for joining poles for shelters, racks and frames. Use rope, wire, paracord or strips of strong fabric.
3. **Digging stick and pry bar:** a hardwood stick sharpened and fire-hardened is a classic survival tool for digging roots and prying.
4. **Containers:** cans, bottles and bags carry water, cook food and store seed. Avoid containers that held chemicals.
5. **Cutting edges:** ATP 3-50.21 shows a stone knife; in practice, scavenged metal and a sharpening stone are faster and safer.
6. **Simple repairs:** tape, wire and lashings fix most tools, shelters and clothing for a while.
7. **Protect yourself:** gloves, eye protection and steady footing while cutting or chopping.

## If / then
- **If** you cut yourself **then** clean and cover the wound right away (fa-wound-care-infection).
- **If** a tool or handle cracks **then** stop using it; a broken tool causes injuries.

## Don't
- Don't use containers that held fuel, pesticide or chemicals for food or water.
- Don't swing axes or hatchets when you are exhausted or in poor light.

## Get help now
Deep cuts, heavy bleeding or a wound that turns red, hot and swollen: fa-severe-bleeding, fa-wound-care-infection, or **911**.
