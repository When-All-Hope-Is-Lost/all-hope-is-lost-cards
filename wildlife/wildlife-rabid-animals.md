## Do this now
**Bitten or scratched by any animal? Wash the wound with soap and running water for 15 minutes**, then get medical care today.

## Steps
1. **Wash right away** with soap and running water **for 15 minutes**.
2. **See a clinician the same day,** and **call your local health department or animal control.** Rabies shots after an exposure prevent the disease, but they must start before symptoms do. Once symptoms start, rabies is almost always fatal.
3. **Tell them** what kind of animal it was, whether it was acting strangely, and where it is now. A pet's owner and vaccination record matter.
4. **Don't try to catch the animal yourself.** Animal control may be able to observe or test it.

## If / then
- **If** you **woke up with a bat in the room**, or a bat touched you or a child, or a bat was near someone who can't tell you what happened **then** get medical advice right away, **even if you see no bite**. Bat bites can be tiny.
- **If** a wild animal is **acting strangely** **then** stay away and call animal control. Signs include unusual aggression or unusual tameness, drooling, trouble swallowing, staggering, paralysis, or a night animal (raccoon, skunk, fox, bat) out in daylight acting oddly.
- **If** your pet tangled with a wild animal **then** don't touch the pet's wounds bare-handed. Call your vet.

## Don't
- Don't touch, feed, or handle wild animals, including baby animals and bats, even dead ones.
- Don't wait to see if you get sick. Treatment must start early.
- Don't let pets roam unvaccinated. Keep their rabies vaccines current.

## Get help now
Any bite or scratch from a wild animal, a bat exposure, or a bite from a stray or unknown pet: **clinic, urgent care, or ER today**, and call the **local health department**. **911** for severe bites.
