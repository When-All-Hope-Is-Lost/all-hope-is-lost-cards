## Do this now
**Remove the tick right away:** grab it **close to the skin** with fine-tipped tweezers and **pull straight out with steady, even pressure.**

## Steps
1. **Don't wait** for a clinic to remove it. Waiting raises the risk of a tickborne disease (CDC).
2. **Grab the tick as close to the skin as possible** with clean, fine-tipped tweezers (regular tweezers or fingers if that's all you have). Don't squeeze its body.
3. **Pull upward with steady, even pressure.** Don't twist or jerk.
4. **If mouthparts stay in the skin,** remove them with tweezers if that's easy. If not, leave them alone. The skin will push them out as it heals.
5. **Clean the bite and your hands** with rubbing alcohol or soap and water.
6. **Get rid of the tick** by flushing it, or keep it in rubbing alcohol or a sealed bag to show a clinician.
7. **Write down the date** and where you think you picked it up.

## If / then
- **If** you get a **rash, fever, chills, tiredness, headache, muscle aches, or joint pain or swelling within about 30 days** **then** see a clinician. Tell them about the bite, when it happened, and where.
- **If** a rash **slowly spreads** (sometimes to 12 inches or more, sometimes with a "bull's-eye" look) **then** it may be Lyme disease. See a clinician. Not every Lyme rash looks like a bull's-eye.
- **If** weakness or paralysis creeps up the body **then** check carefully for an attached tick. Removing it usually helps within 24 hours, but get emergency care.
- **If** you live where Lyme disease is common **then** ask a clinician whether a single preventive antibiotic dose makes sense.

## Don't
- **Don't use nail polish, petroleum jelly, or a hot match** to make a tick let go.
- Don't twist, jerk, or crush the tick.
- Don't ignore a fever or rash in the weeks after a bite. Most tickborne diseases are treatable with antibiotics.

## Get help now
Fever with a spreading rash, severe headache with a stiff neck, facial drooping, a racing or irregular heartbeat, or creeping weakness after a tick bite: get medical care **today** (911 if severe).
