## Do this now
**Don't run.** Stand still, stay calm, and turn your side toward the dog. Say "No!" or "Go home!" in a deep, firm voice, then back away slowly.

## Steps
1. **An unfamiliar dog (or several) approaches:** stay still and calm, and **don't look it in the eye**. Stand with the side of your body facing it, and slowly bring your hands up to your neck with your elbows in.
2. Say **"No"** or **"Go home"** in a firm, deep voice. Wait for it to pass, or **back away slowly**.
3. **Put something between you and the dog:** a bag, purse, or jacket (CDC).
4. **Knocked down?** **Curl into a ball**, tuck your head, and put your hands over your ears and neck.
5. **Coyote approaches or follows you:** **don't run**, because running can trigger a chase. Wave your arms and yell, and use an air horn or bang pots if you have them. Throw small sticks or stones **toward** it (not at it to hurt it). Keep scaring it until it has completely left. If it comes near a child, yell, move toward the coyote, pick the child up, and back away.

## If / then
- **If** you're bitten or scratched **then** **wash the wound with soap and running water for 15 minutes**, get medical care, and report the bite to animal control or the health department (see the rabid-animals card).
- **If** you see loose or roaming dogs **then** don't approach them, even friendly-looking ones. Call animal control.
- **If** coyotes hang around your home **then** don't feed them, secure trash and pet food, and keep pets and small animals inside at night.

## Don't
- Don't run, scream, or panic around a threatening dog.
- Don't try to break up a dog fight with your hands.
- Don't feed coyotes or stray dog packs. Feeding makes them bolder.

## Get help now
**911** for serious bites (deep wounds, heavy bleeding, bites to the face or neck, or attacks on children). For any bite, see a clinician about rabies and infection, and report it to animal control.
