## Do this now
**Cook every insect you eat thoroughly, and skip any that are brightly colored, hairy, stinging, or smell bad.**

## Steps
1. **Usually safe once cooked (US Army survival manual):** grasshoppers and crickets (remove legs and wings), beetle grubs, ants, termites, earthworms (purge, then cook).
2. **Cook them:** boil or roast until fully done. Raw insects can carry parasites and germs.
3. **Avoid:** brightly colored, hairy or fuzzy insects (caterpillars), anything that stings or bites, ticks, flies, mosquitoes, and insects found on dead animals or waste.
4. **Allergy:** people allergic to **shellfish or dust mites** may react to insects (shared protein, FAO 2013). Try a small amount first, and not at all if you've had a severe shellfish reaction.

## If / then
- **If** bitten by a **black widow** (shiny black, red hourglass underneath) **then** wash, cold pack, and get medical care; severe cramping, sweating or breathing trouble means 911.
- **If** bitten by a **brown recluse** (brown, violin mark, in dry closets and sheds) **then** wash, cold pack, and see a clinician; a spreading sore or fever needs care.
- **If** you find a **tick** **then** pull it straight out with fine tweezers close to the skin and wash. Rash, fever, aches in the next weeks: see a clinician and say you had a tick (Lyme, Rocky Mountain spotted fever, others). See the Ticks card.
- **If** stung by **fire ants** **then** move away, wash, cold pack, don't pop the pustules.
- **If** after any sting there is swelling of the face or throat, hives all over, wheezing, or fainting **then** use an epinephrine auto-injector if available and **call 911**: anaphylaxis.
- **If** a bee stinger is in the skin **then** scrape or pull it out quickly.

## Don't
- Don't eat insects raw or ones you can't identify.
- Don't put heat, ice directly, or cut a spider bite.
- Don't use matches, petroleum jelly or nail polish on ticks.

## Get help now
Emergency **911**. Poison Help **1-800-222-1222** (bites and stings too).
