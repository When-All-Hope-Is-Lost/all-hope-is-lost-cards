## Do this now
**Snake bite: call 911 or get to an ER now.** Keep calm and still, take off rings and watches, and **don't** cut, suck, ice, or tie off the bite.

## Steps
**Snake bite**
1. **Get emergency care right away**, even if it doesn't hurt much yet. Antivenom may be needed. **Don't drive yourself**, since the bite can make you dizzy or pass out.
2. **Lie or sit down**, with the bitten part in a neutral, comfortable position (about heart level). Move as little as possible.
3. **Take off rings, watches, and tight clothing** before swelling starts.
4. **Wash the bite with soap and water** and cover it with a clean, dry dressing.
5. **Mark the edge of the swelling** on the skin with a pen, and write the time next to it.
6. **Snap a photo of the snake from a safe distance** if you can. Don't try to catch it.

**Spider bite**
7. Stay calm. Identify the spider only if it's safe to do so.
8. **Wash the bite with soap and water.** Put a cold, wet cloth or wrapped ice on it to reduce swelling, and raise it if you can.
9. **Get medical care.**

## If / then
- **If** you're in Missouri **then** the five venomous snakes are all pit vipers: the **copperhead** (the most common; hourglass-shaped bands), the **cottonmouth** (water moccasin; wetlands and streams in the south and southeast), and the **timber**, **western pygmy**, and **massasauga rattlesnakes**. They have a heat-sensing pit between the eye and nostril, and vertical pupils.
- **If** a **brown recluse** may have bitten you (a brown spider with a dark violin shape; found in closets, shoes, attics, and woodpiles) **then** watch for a blister or sore that spreads, and see a clinician. The venom can destroy skin.
- **If** a **black widow** bit you, and the pain spreads to the belly, chest, or whole body **then** get emergency care.
- **If** the person gets pale, faint, or confused **then** treat for shock and call 911.

## Don't
- **Don't use a tourniquet, cut the wound, or try to suck out the venom.**
- **Don't put ice on a snake bite** or soak it in water. Don't drink alcohol, and don't take aspirin, ibuprofen, or naproxen.
- **Don't handle any venomous snake, even a dead one or its cut-off head.** Most bites happen when people try to catch or kill snakes. In Missouri, native snakes are protected by law.

## Get help now
**911** for any venomous snake bite, or for spider bites with spreading pain, cramps, trouble breathing, or a spreading sore. **Poison Help 1-800-222-1222** for advice.
