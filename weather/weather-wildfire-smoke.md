## Do this now
**Told to evacuate? Leave immediately.** Trapped? **Call 911**, give your location, and turn on lights so rescuers can find you. In smoke, stay indoors in a closed-up room, or wear an **N95** mask.

## Steps
1. **Watch alerts** (Wireless Emergency Alerts, local officials) and **air quality** (the AirNow Fire and Smoke Map).
2. **Evacuate right away when told.** Know your routes and where you'll go, and take your pets.
3. **If you're not ordered out but it's smoky:** stay inside with the doors and windows closed. Make one **"clean air room"** and run a portable air cleaner in it.
4. **Central air?** Use a high-efficiency filter (MERV-13 or higher), set the fan to "On" and "Recirculate," and close the outdoor air intake.
5. **Cut down on indoor smoke:** don't fry food, vacuum, sweep, or burn gas, propane, or wood.
6. **Outside in smoke?** Take it easy, limit your time, and wear an **N95 respirator**. **Cloth masks don't protect against smoke.**

## If / then
- **If** you're trapped **then** call 911 and give your location, but know that help may be delayed or impossible. Turn on lights to help rescuers find you.
- **If** your indoor air gets too hot **then** go to a clean-air shelter or another air-conditioned building with good filtration, or stay with friends or family.
- **If** you're returning home **then** wait until authorities say it's safe. Watch for hot ash, smoldering debris, and **heat pockets in the ground** that can burn you. Wear long sleeves, long pants, gloves, thick-soled shoes, and a respirator, and wet down debris to keep dust down.
- **If** you have asthma, COPD, or other lung problems **then** take extra care in poor air, because smoke can make symptoms worse.

## Don't
- **Don't wait to leave once you're told to evacuate.**
- Don't rely on cloth masks for smoke.
- Don't go home before officials say it's safe.

## Get help now
**911** if you're trapped or someone is hurt. Prepare: keep a **30-foot zone around your home** clear of leaves and debris, keep a hose that reaches all sides of the house, and store N95 masks.

**Outside the US** (official sites, checked 2026-10-16):
- **Emergency number:** UK **999 or 112** · Ireland **112 or 999** · Canada **911** (Nunavut and a few other places use local numbers) · Australia **000** (112 also works from a mobile; you can't text 000) · New Zealand **111**.
- **Official warnings:** UK **Met Office** (yellow, amber, red) · Ireland **Met Éireann** (met.ie) · Canada **Environment and Climate Change Canada** (weather.gc.ca or the WeatherCAN app; yellow, orange, or red Warnings, Advisories, and Watches) · Australia **Bureau of Meteorology** (bom.gov.au), plus state emergency services (Advice, Watch and Act, Emergency Warning) · New Zealand **MetService** (metservice.com; Watches, then Orange and Red Warnings), plus local Civil Defence.
- **Terms:** Australia says **bushfire**. The Bureau of Meteorology issues Fire Weather Warnings, and state fire services issue Advice, Watch and Act, and Emergency Warnings. Canada and NZ say wildfire or vegetation fire.
