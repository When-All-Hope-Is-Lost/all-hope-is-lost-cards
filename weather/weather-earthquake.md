## Do this now
**Drop, Cover, and Hold On** right where you are, until the shaking stops.

## Steps
1. **Drop** onto your hands and knees.
2. **Cover** your head and neck with one arm. Crawl under a sturdy table or desk if one is close. If not, get next to an interior wall away from windows. Stay low and bend over.
3. **Hold on** until the shaking stops. If you're under something, hold it with one hand and move with it.
4. **Inside? Stay inside.** Don't run outside, and stay out of doorways.
5. **In bed?** Turn face down and cover your head and neck with a pillow.
6. **Outside?** Stay outside, move to an open area away from buildings, trees, streetlights, and power lines, then drop and cover.
7. **Driving?** Pull over, stop, and set the parking brake.

## If / then
- **If** you use a wheelchair or walker **then** lock your wheels, get as low as you can, cover your head and neck, and hold on.
- **If** your building is damaged **then** get outside and away from it, and don't go back into damaged buildings. Expect **aftershocks**, and drop, cover, and hold on again when one hits.
- **If** you're trapped **then** text for help, or bang on a pipe or wall. Use a whistle instead of shouting, and cover your mouth with your shirt.
- **If** you smell gas or see damaged lines **then** see the gas-leak card.

## Don't
- Don't run outside during the shaking, or stand in a doorway.
- Don't try to move heavy debris by yourself.
- Don't use the phone for calls unless it's an emergency. **Texts get through more reliably and save battery.**

## Get help now
**911** for injuries or people trapped.
**Where:** USGS's 2023 model says **nearly 75% of the US** could see potentially damaging shaking within 100 years. The highest hazard is along the West Coast, in Alaska, and in Hawaii. The central US also has a significant hazard zone, the **New Madrid seismic zone**, which produced three earthquakes of about magnitude 7.3 to 7.5 in 1811–1812. USGS estimates a **7–10% chance** of a repeat of those quakes **in any 50-year period** there. **Near a coast?** If shaking is strong or long, move to high ground once it stops (see the tsunami card). **Prepare:** secure bookcases, water heaters, and TVs, and keep supplies for several days.

**Outside the US** (official sites, checked 2026-10-16):
- **Emergency number:** UK **999 or 112** · Ireland **112 or 999** · Canada **911** (Nunavut and a few other places use local numbers) · Australia **000** (112 also works from a mobile; you can't text 000) · New Zealand **111**.
- **Official warnings:** UK **Met Office** (yellow, amber, red) · Ireland **Met Éireann** (met.ie) · Canada **Environment and Climate Change Canada** (weather.gc.ca or the WeatherCAN app; yellow, orange, or red Warnings, Advisories, and Watches) · Australia **Bureau of Meteorology** (bom.gov.au), plus state emergency services (Advice, Watch and Act, Emergency Warning) · New Zealand **MetService** (metservice.com; Watches, then Orange and Red Warnings), plus local Civil Defence.
- **Terms:** New Zealand and western Canada (British Columbia) have significant earthquake hazard. Follow your national civil defence or emergency management agency.
