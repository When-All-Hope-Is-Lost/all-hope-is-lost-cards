## Do this now
**When thunder roars, go indoors.** Get into a **building or a hard-topped car**. Stay there until **30 minutes after the last thunder**.

## Steps
1. **Get inside** a sturdy building or an enclosed vehicle with a roof. **Tents, picnic shelters, dugouts, and open-sided shelters are not safe.**
2. **Indoors:** stay off corded phones and devices plugged into outlets, and avoid running water (showers, washing dishes).
3. **Wait 30 minutes after the last rumble of thunder** before going back outside.
4. **Watch for flash flooding.** **Turn around, don't drown.**
5. **After the storm:** stay clear of fallen trees and power lines, and report them right away.

## If / then
- **If** you absolutely **can't reach** a building or car **then** know that you're **not safe**, but you can lower the risk:
  - Get off hilltops, ridges, and open fields.
  - Stay away from tall, isolated trees and other tall objects.
  - Stay away from water, wet ropes, and metal such as fences and poles.
  - **In a group, spread out.**
- **If** you're on the water **then** head for land as soon as storms threaten. If you can't, drop anchor and get as low as you can. A boat with a cabin is safer; stay inside the cabin.
- **If** someone is struck by lightning **then** call **911**. If they're not breathing, start CPR (see the cardiac arrest card).
- **If** a severe thunderstorm warning is issued **then** go inside and follow alerts. These storms can bring **intense wind, hail, flash flooding**, and sometimes tornadoes.

## Don't
- Don't shelter under a lone tree, a tent, or an open picnic shelter.
- Don't go back outside right after the rain stops. Wait the full 30 minutes.
- Don't drive through flooded roads.

## Get help now
**911.** Lightning kills about **20 people a year** in the US and injures hundreds more, and some survivors have lifelong nerve damage. Get alerts from **NOAA Weather Radio**, the Emergency Alert System, and your community's warning system.

**Outside the US** (official sites, checked 2026-10-16):
- **Emergency number:** UK **999 or 112** · Ireland **112 or 999** · Canada **911** (Nunavut and a few other places use local numbers) · Australia **000** (112 also works from a mobile; you can't text 000) · New Zealand **111**.
- **Official warnings:** UK **Met Office** (yellow, amber, red) · Ireland **Met Éireann** (met.ie) · Canada **Environment and Climate Change Canada** (weather.gc.ca or the WeatherCAN app; yellow, orange, or red Warnings, Advisories, and Watches) · Australia **Bureau of Meteorology** (bom.gov.au), plus state emergency services (Advice, Watch and Act, Emergency Warning) · New Zealand **MetService** (metservice.com; Watches, then Orange and Red Warnings), plus local Civil Defence.
- **Terms:** The Met Office issues separate thunderstorm and lightning warnings. In Canada, the most severe thunderstorm warnings (very strong winds or large hail) are sent through Alert Ready.
