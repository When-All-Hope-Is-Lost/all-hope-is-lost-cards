## Do this now
**Dust blowing across the road ahead? Pull off the pavement as far as you can, stop, turn off ALL your lights, set the parking brake, and take your foot off the brake.**

## Steps
1. **Don't drive into a dust storm** if you can avoid it.
2. **Get off the road:** pull completely off the pavement, as far as you can.
3. **Lights off** (headlights and tail lights), parking brake on, **foot off the brake pedal**. Drivers behind you may follow your lights and hit you.
4. **Wait it out.** Dust storms usually last only a few minutes.

## If / then
- **If** you **can't** pull off **then** slow to a speed that fits what you can see, turn your lights on, and sound your horn now and then. Use the painted center line as a guide, and look for a safe place to pull off.
- **If** a **Dust Storm Warning** is issued for your area **then** delay travel if you can.

## Don't
- **Never stop in a travel lane.**
- **Don't leave your lights on once you've parked off the road.**
- Don't follow the lights of the car ahead to find your way off the road. Drivers doing that have run off the road or hit parked cars.

## Get help now
**911** for crashes. Dust storms can happen anywhere in the US but are most common in the **Southwest**, often from thunderstorm outflow winds. They arrive suddenly as a wall of dust that can be miles long, and they can cause chain-reaction pileups.

**Outside the US** (official sites, checked 2026-10-16):
- **Emergency number:** UK **999 or 112** · Ireland **112 or 999** · Canada **911** (Nunavut and a few other places use local numbers) · Australia **000** (112 also works from a mobile; you can't text 000) · New Zealand **111**.
- **Official warnings:** UK **Met Office** (yellow, amber, red) · Ireland **Met Éireann** (met.ie) · Canada **Environment and Climate Change Canada** (weather.gc.ca or the WeatherCAN app; yellow, orange, or red Warnings, Advisories, and Watches) · Australia **Bureau of Meteorology** (bom.gov.au), plus state emergency services (Advice, Watch and Act, Emergency Warning) · New Zealand **MetService** (metservice.com; Watches, then Orange and Red Warnings), plus local Civil Defence.
- **Terms:** Australia also has dust storms. Check Bureau of Meteorology warnings.
