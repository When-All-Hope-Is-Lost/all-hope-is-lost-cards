## Do this now
**If officials tell you to evacuate, go now.** If you're staying, shelter in an **interior room** away from windows. **Storm surge** (seawater pushed ashore) is historically the biggest killer.

## Steps
1. **Know your evacuation zone** and route. Plan where you'll go, and include your pets.
2. **When an evacuation is ordered for your zone, leave immediately.**
3. **Before the storm:** charge your phone and backup batteries, bring in outdoor furniture, clear drains and gutters, and pack medicine and pet supplies. You may not be able to get supplies for days or weeks.
4. **During the storm:** stay in a designated storm shelter or an **interior room** for the high winds.
5. **Trapped by rising water?** Go to the **highest level** of the building, but **not into a closed attic**, where you could be trapped.
6. **Get alerts several ways:** Wireless Emergency Alerts, the Emergency Alert System, NOAA Weather Radio, and local officials.

## If / then
- **If** you live inland **then** still prepare. Hurricanes bring rain, flooding, wind, and even tornadoes far from where they make landfall.
- **If** you come to floodwater **then** **turn around, don't drown.** Six inches of moving water can knock you down, and one foot can sweep a car away.
- **If** the power is out afterward **then** use generators only outdoors (see the power outage and carbon monoxide cards).
- **If** you're cleaning up **then** wear protective clothing and a mask for mold, and work with someone else. People with asthma, lung disease, or weak immune systems should stay out of moldy buildings, and children shouldn't help with cleanup.

## Don't
- **Don't walk, swim, or drive through floodwater.** It can hide debris and germs, and it can be **electrified by downed lines**.
- Don't touch electrical equipment if it's wet or you're standing in water.
- Don't make phone calls unless it's an emergency. **Text** family instead.

## Get help now
**911** for emergencies. **Season:** the Atlantic season runs June 1 to November 30, and the Eastern Pacific season May 15 to November 30. In the western North Pacific these storms are called **typhoons**. FEMA Helpline: **1-800-621-3362**.

**Outside the US** (official sites, checked 2026-10-16):
- **Emergency number:** UK **999 or 112** · Ireland **112 or 999** · Canada **911** (Nunavut and a few other places use local numbers) · Australia **000** (112 also works from a mobile; you can't text 000) · New Zealand **111**.
- **Official warnings:** UK **Met Office** (yellow, amber, red) · Ireland **Met Éireann** (met.ie) · Canada **Environment and Climate Change Canada** (weather.gc.ca or the WeatherCAN app; yellow, orange, or red Warnings, Advisories, and Watches) · Australia **Bureau of Meteorology** (bom.gov.au), plus state emergency services (Advice, Watch and Act, Emergency Warning) · New Zealand **MetService** (metservice.com; Watches, then Orange and Red Warnings), plus local Civil Defence.
- **Terms:** Australia and the South Pacific say **tropical cyclone** (Bureau of Meteorology issues cyclone watches and warnings). NZ gets **ex-tropical cyclones** (MetService Red Warnings). Canada has the Canadian Hurricane Centre. 'Typhoon' is used in the western North Pacific.
