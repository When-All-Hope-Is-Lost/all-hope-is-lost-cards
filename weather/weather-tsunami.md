## Do this now
**On the coast and feel a strong earthquake, hear a roar from the ocean, or see the sea suddenly rise or drain away? Go to high ground or far inland now.** **Don't wait** for an official warning.

## Steps
1. **If the ground is shaking, protect yourself first:** Drop, Cover, and Hold On.
2. **When the shaking stops, go** as **high and as far inland** as you can. Aim for **100 feet (30 m) above sea level or at least 1 mile (1.6 km) inland**.
3. **Follow evacuation route signs** (a wave with an arrow pointing to higher ground).
4. **Leave immediately** if officials tell you to. If you're **outside** the hazard zone and a warning comes, **stay where you are** unless told otherwise.
5. **Stay away** until officials say it's safe. A tsunami is a **series** of waves, and they can keep coming.

## If / then
- **If** you're in the water **then** grab something that floats, such as a raft or a tree trunk.
- **If** you're on a boat **then** face the waves and head out to sea. **If** you're in a harbor **then** go inland.
- **If** you're coming back afterward **then** stay away from damaged buildings, roads, and bridges. Avoid floodwater: it can be deeper than it looks, hide debris, or be **electrified** by downed lines.

## Don't
- **Don't go back to the coast until officials say it's safe.**
- Don't wait for an official warning if you see the natural signs.
- Don't make phone calls unless it's an emergency. **Text** instead.

## Get help now
**911.** Tsunami waves can travel **20 to 30 mph** and be **10 to 100 feet** high. **Pacific and Caribbean coasts** have the highest US risk. Sign up for local alerts, and learn your community's evacuation map.

**Outside the US** (official sites, checked 2026-10-16):
- **Emergency number:** UK **999 or 112** · Ireland **112 or 999** · Canada **911** (Nunavut and a few other places use local numbers) · Australia **000** (112 also works from a mobile; you can't text 000) · New Zealand **111**.
- **Official warnings:** UK **Met Office** (yellow, amber, red) · Ireland **Met Éireann** (met.ie) · Canada **Environment and Climate Change Canada** (weather.gc.ca or the WeatherCAN app; yellow, orange, or red Warnings, Advisories, and Watches) · Australia **Bureau of Meteorology** (bom.gov.au), plus state emergency services (Advice, Watch and Act, Emergency Warning) · New Zealand **MetService** (metservice.com; Watches, then Orange and Red Warnings), plus local Civil Defence.
- **Terms:** NZ and Australia have national tsunami warnings through their civil defence and emergency agencies (NZ Civil Defence; Australia via the Bureau of Meteorology). The NZ message 'Long or strong, get gone' means leave the coast after a long or strong earthquake (verify).
