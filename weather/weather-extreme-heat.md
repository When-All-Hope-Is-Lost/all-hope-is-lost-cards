## Do this now
**Get somewhere cool (air conditioning, or a cooling center), drink fluids, and check on anyone at risk.** Hot, red, dry skin, confusion, or passing out means **heat stroke: call 911.**

## Steps
1. **Get cool.** No air conditioning at home? Go to a **cooling center**, library, or mall. Ask your local health department where to go.
2. **Don't rely on a fan alone.** Fans move air but don't lower your body temperature or prevent heat illness.
3. **Drink plenty of fluids.** Take cool showers or baths.
4. **Wear loose, lightweight, light-colored clothing.** Outdoors, stay in the shade and wear a wide-brimmed hat.
5. **Avoid hard work or exercise in the midday heat.** Use your oven less.
6. **Check on** older adults, children, people with chronic illness, and neighbors. They're at higher risk.
7. **Pets need cool water and shade.** Hot pavement can burn their paws.

## If / then
- **If** someone has a **temperature above 103°F, red, hot, dry skin, a fast strong pulse, dizziness, confusion, or passes out** **then** it's **heat stroke**. Call **911**. While you wait, move them somewhere cool, take off extra clothing, and put cold wet cloths or ice packs on the head, neck, armpits, and groin (or soak their clothes with cool water).
- **If** someone has **heavy sweating, paleness, cramps, weakness, headache, nausea, or fainting** (heat exhaustion) **then** move them somewhere cooler, loosen or remove extra clothing, and give sips of water or a sports drink. Call a doctor if it gets worse or lasts more than an hour.
- **If** the power goes out in a heat wave **then** see the power outage card and head to a cooling location.

## Don't
- **Never leave a child, an adult, or a pet in a closed car** on a warm day.
- Don't count on a fan as your main cooling.
- Don't do strenuous work in the midday sun if you can avoid it.

## Get help now
**911** for heat stroke. Ask your local health department about cooling centers. Help paying cooling bills: the Low Income Home Energy Assistance Program (**LIHEAP**).

**Outside the US** (official sites, checked 2026-10-16):
- **Emergency number:** UK **999 or 112** · Ireland **112 or 999** · Canada **911** (Nunavut and a few other places use local numbers) · Australia **000** (112 also works from a mobile; you can't text 000) · New Zealand **111**.
- **Official warnings:** UK **Met Office** (yellow, amber, red) · Ireland **Met Éireann** (met.ie) · Canada **Environment and Climate Change Canada** (weather.gc.ca or the WeatherCAN app; yellow, orange, or red Warnings, Advisories, and Watches) · Australia **Bureau of Meteorology** (bom.gov.au), plus state emergency services (Advice, Watch and Act, Emergency Warning) · New Zealand **MetService** (metservice.com; Watches, then Orange and Red Warnings), plus local Civil Defence.
- **Terms:** UK: the Met Office issues Extreme Heat warnings, and England also has separate Heat-Health Alerts. 'Heatwave' is the usual spelling outside the US.
