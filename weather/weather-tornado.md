## Do this now
**Go now** to a basement, safe room, or a small interior room with no windows on the lowest floor. **Cover your head and neck.**

## Steps
1. **Move immediately** to your safe place: a FEMA safe room or storm shelter if you have one. Otherwise go to a **basement**, or a **small, windowless interior room on the lowest floor** of a sturdy building.
2. **Stay away from windows, doors, and outside walls.**
3. **Protect your head and neck** with your arms. Pile blankets, cushions, or furniture around or on top of you.
4. **Keep listening** to the Emergency Alert System, NOAA Weather Radio, or local alerts.
5. **Can't stay at home?** Go to a public shelter.

## If / then
- **If** you're in a **car or truck** **then** know there's no safe option, only less dangerous ones.
  - If the tornado is visible and far away and traffic is light, drive away from its path at a right angle.
  - If debris is flying, pull off the road. Stay in the car with your seat belt on and your head below the windows, covered with your hands and a coat or blanket.
  - If there's ground clearly lower than the road, you can lie there instead and cover your head.
- **If** you see a rotating funnel, an approaching debris cloud, or hear a loud roar like a freight train **then** take shelter immediately, even without a warning.
- **If** the tornado has passed **then** stay clear of downed power lines and broken utility lines. Text rather than call, so lines stay open for emergencies.

## Don't
- **Don't shelter under a highway overpass or bridge.** It gives little protection and creates deadly traffic hazards.
- Don't stay in a room with windows, or near outside walls.
- Don't go near fallen power lines or broken utility lines.

## Get help now
**911** for injuries or people trapped. For cleanup, wear thick-soled shoes, long pants, and work gloves.

**Outside the US** (official sites, checked 2026-10-16):
- **Emergency number:** UK **999 or 112** · Ireland **112 or 999** · Canada **911** (Nunavut and a few other places use local numbers) · Australia **000** (112 also works from a mobile; you can't text 000) · New Zealand **111**.
- **Official warnings:** UK **Met Office** (yellow, amber, red) · Ireland **Met Éireann** (met.ie) · Canada **Environment and Climate Change Canada** (weather.gc.ca or the WeatherCAN app; yellow, orange, or red Warnings, Advisories, and Watches) · Australia **Bureau of Meteorology** (bom.gov.au), plus state emergency services (Advice, Watch and Act, Emergency Warning) · New Zealand **MetService** (metservice.com; Watches, then Orange and Red Warnings), plus local Civil Defence.
- **Terms:** Canada issues tornado warnings through Environment and Climate Change Canada, and the most urgent ones go out through the national Alert Ready system.
