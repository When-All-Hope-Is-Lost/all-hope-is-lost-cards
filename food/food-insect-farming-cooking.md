## Do this now
**Start with mealworms or crickets in a ventilated bin, feed them clean food, and always cook them thoroughly before eating.**

## Steps
1. **Housing:**
   - **Mealworms:** smooth-sided plastic bin, 2 to 3 inches of bran or oats as bedding and food, holes or mesh lid. Easiest to keep.
   - **Crickets:** tall bin or tub with mesh lid, egg cartons for hiding, shallow dish of damp soil or coconut fiber for egg laying. Thailand farmers use concrete rings or boxes with egg trays (FAO 2013).
   - **Black soldier fly larvae:** bin of food scraps, best as chicken or fish feed.
2. **Warmth (estimate):** most grow best around 75 to 90°F; cooler means slower.
3. **Feed:** grain, bran, vegetable scraps, carrot or potato slices for moisture (mealworms); chicken feed and greens, water on a sponge or gel (crickets; standing water drowns them). No moldy food or pesticides.
4. **Harvest (estimate):** mealworms as larvae before they pupate, roughly 2 to 3 months; crickets near adult size, roughly 6 to 8 weeks. Leave some to breed.
5. **Purge:** move harvested insects to a clean container with only fresh vegetables or nothing for about a day, so their guts empty.
6. **Kill humanely:** put them in the freezer until dead (a few hours).
7. **Cook:** rinse, then boil several minutes or roast until dry and crisp. Never eat raw.
8. **Store:** cooked and dried, in a sealed jar, cool and dry; or freeze. Grind dried insects into flour for soups and bread.

## If / then
- **If** you're allergic to shellfish or dust mites **then** be very cautious or don't eat them (FAO 2013).
- **If** the colony smells sour or molds **then** clean it out and reduce moisture.
- **If** wheezing, swelling, or hives after eating **then** call 911 (anaphylaxis).

## Don't
- Don't feed them spoiled food, meat or manure if you plan to eat them.
- Don't eat insects that died on their own before you harvested them.
- Don't release farmed insects outdoors.
