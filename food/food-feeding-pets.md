## Do this now
**Ask for help early:** pet food pantries, low-cost vet care, or 2-1-1, before a crisis forces a hard choice.

## Steps
1. **Rough food cost (2021 estimate):** ASPCA's cost table lists food at about **$300 a year for a dog** and **$225 a year for a cat**, roughly $25 and $19 a month. Real costs vary a lot by size, diet, and today's prices.
2. **Find pet food help:** local shelters, community clinics, **2-1-1**, and Humane World for Animals' "Having trouble affording your pet" resource list. Some human food banks stock pet food, so ask.
3. **Low-cost spay and neuter:** ASPCA's low-cost program finder (aspca.org).
4. **Feed a complete commercial pet food** and don't overfeed. ASPCA notes this is often more cost-effective than homemade food.
5. **If you truly can't care for your pet:** rehoming is the humane option. Ask friends and family first, then reputable rescues and shelters about rehoming or surrender. Many shelters offer intake appointments or temporary help.

## If / then
- **If** you're losing your housing **then** ask shelters about pet-friendly options or temporary foster programs.
- **If** your pet is sick and you can't pay **then** call local shelters, clinics, or a veterinary school about payment plans or help **before** waiting it out.

## Don't
- **Never abandon a pet** or turn it loose outside. It's inhumane and often illegal.
- Never harm an animal. If euthanasia ever comes up for a sick animal, that's a decision for a licensed veterinarian.
- Don't feed pets foods that are toxic to them (see the ASPCA Animal Poison Control list online).

## Get help now
Pet emergency you can't afford: local shelters, vet schools, or **2-1-1**. You, hungry: the food-today card.
