## Do this now
**Store at least 1 gallon of water per person per day,** and food your household will actually eat that needs no fridge or cooking. Start with a few days' worth, then build toward **two weeks** for sheltering at home.

## Steps
1. **Water: count it out.**
   - Ready.gov: **at least 1 gallon per person per day for several days,** for drinking and sanitation.
   - FEMA and the VA: for a longer stay at home, aim for a **two-week supply**.
   - **Formula:** people × days × 1 gallon. One person for 14 days = **14 gallons**. Four people for 3 days = 12 gallons. Four people for 14 days = 56 gallons.
   - Store more for children, nursing mothers, sick people, and hot weather. In very hot temperatures, water needs can double (Ready.gov).
2. **Food: how long.** Ready.gov says **at least a several-day supply** of non-perishable food. FEMA suggests considering a supply that lasts **two weeks**.
3. **Food: what to stock.** Pick foods that need no fridge, cooking, water, or special preparation (Ready.gov), for example:
   - canned soup, canned meat or fish, canned beans
   - canned fruit (in juice) and canned vegetables
   - peanut butter
   - rice and oats (these need water and heat; see If / then)
   - dry cereal or granola, protein or fruit bars, crackers
   - non-perishable milk, such as **shelf-stable milk**
   - food for infants, pets, and special diets
   - a **manual can opener** and eating utensils
4. **One example for a single adult, two weeks (VA guide, estimate):** 14 gallons of water; 28 cans or pouches of meat, fish, or entrees; 24 cans of fruit; 24 cans of vegetables or beans; 2 small jars of peanut butter; crackers, bars, and powdered or shelf-stable milk. Treat it as a guide, not a quota.
5. **Build it slowly.** You don't have to buy it all at once. Add a few low-cost shelf-stable items on each regular grocery trip, cheapest per serving first. FEMA notes you may already have much of it in your cupboards.
6. **Store it right.**
   - Water: keep store-bought water **sealed in its original container** in a cool, dark place. Replace water you bottled yourself **every six months** (Ready.gov). Use only food-grade containers.
   - Cans: keep them cool, clean, and dry, ideally below 85°F (USDA).
7. **Rotate: first in, first out.** Put new cans behind the old ones and eat the oldest first. Check dates. As a quality guide (USDA): high-acid cans like tomatoes and fruit keep about **12–18 months**; low-acid cans like meat, fish, and most vegetables keep about **2–5 years** if the can stays intact.

## If / then
- **If** you have little space **then** store as much as you can (FEMA).
- **If** your water could run low **then** choose canned foods with high liquid content and skip salty foods, which make you thirsty (FEMA). Rice, pasta, and oats need water to cook.
- **If** you have to evacuate **then** take a grab-and-go **3-day** supply (Red Cross: 3 days for evacuation, 2 weeks at home).
- **If** a can is swollen, dented, leaking, or corroded **then** throw it out, even if the food looks fine (Ready.gov, USDA).
- **If** money is tight **then** see the budget-eating card and call **2-1-1** for food help.

## Don't
- Don't ration drinking water unless officials tell you to. Drink what you need today and look for more tomorrow (Ready.gov).
- Don't count soda, coffee, or alcohol as your drinking water. Caffeine and alcohol increase how much water you need (Ready.gov).
- Don't buy foods nobody in your home will eat.
- Don't forget the manual can opener.

## Get help now
**2-1-1** for food pantries and local help. **911** for emergencies. Red Cross preparedness list: redcross.org/get-help/how-to-prepare-for-emergencies/survival-kit-supplies.html
