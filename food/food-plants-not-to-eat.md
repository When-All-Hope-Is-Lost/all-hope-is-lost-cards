## Do this now
**If you can't name a plant with certainty from a trusted local guide, don't eat it.** Several of the deadliest plants look like carrots, parsnips, wild onions or berries.

## Steps
1. **Learn these first. All parts can be dangerous unless noted.**
   - **Water hemlock** (*Cicuta*): carrot family, wet ground. Toxin: cicutoxin. Seizures, can kill quickly. Looks like wild carrot, parsnip, celery.
   - **Poison hemlock** (*Conium maculatum*): carrot family, purple-blotched hairless stems, musty smell. Toxin: coniine. Paralysis, breathing stops. Looks like wild carrot (Queen Anne's lace), parsley, fennel.
   - **Death camas** (*Toxicoscordion*/*Zigadenus*): grass-like leaves, bulb **with no onion smell**. Toxin: zygacine (steroidal alkaloids). Vomiting, slow heart, collapse. Looks like wild onion and camas.
   - **Jimsonweed** (*Datura stramonium*): spiny seed pods, trumpet flowers. Tropane alkaloids (atropine, scopolamine). Hot, dry, flushed, confused, hallucinating, racing heart.
   - **Pokeweed** (*Phytolacca americana*): purple berries, magenta stems. Root most toxic; berries and mature plant toxic. Vomiting, diarrhea. Traditional "poke sallet" requires young leaves and repeated boiling; raw is poisonous.
   - **Nightshades** (*Solanum* spp., e.g., bittersweet, black nightshade): unripe berries and leaves. Solanine-type glycoalkaloids. Stomach upset, drowsiness. Green potato skins and sprouts carry the same type of toxin.
   - **Foxglove** (*Digitalis*) and **lily of the valley**: cardiac glycosides. Nausea, vision changes, dangerous heart rhythms. Foxglove leaves can be mistaken for comfrey.
   - **Oleander** (*Nerium oleander*): ornamental in the South and West. Cardiac glycosides. Don't burn it or cook over its sticks.
   - **Castor bean** (*Ricinus communis*): spiny pods, mottled seeds. Ricin; chewed seeds can kill.
   - **Yew** (*Taxus*): evergreen with red berry-like cones. Seeds and needles contain taxine; can stop the heart. The red flesh around the seed is the only part not toxic, and the seed inside is; don't risk it.
2. **Mushrooms:** Death cap and destroying angel (*Amanita*) cause most mushroom deaths. Symptoms often start **6 or more hours later**, then seem to improve while the liver fails. No field test (silver, garlic, peeling, animals eating it) shows a mushroom is safe.
3. **Warning signs used by the US military survival manual** (rules of thumb, not proof of safety): milky sap, umbrella-shaped flower clusters (carrot family), almond smell in crushed leaves, three-leaved growth, spines or fine hairs, bean/pod seeds, bitter or soapy taste.

## If / then
- **If** someone ate any plant above or an unknown wild plant or mushroom **then** call Poison Help **1-800-222-1222** now, even with no symptoms. See the "Swallowed poison" card.
- **If** there are seizures, trouble breathing, collapse or a very slow or irregular pulse **then call 911.**
- **If** possible **then** keep a sample of the plant or mushroom (in a paper bag) and take it to care.

## Don't
- Don't rely on "animals eat it" or taste tests to decide.
- Don't make teas or smoke from unknown plants.
- Don't eat any carrot-family wild plant unless an expert confirmed it in person.

## Get help now
Poison Help **1-800-222-1222** (24/7, free). Emergency **911**. County extension office for plant ID.
