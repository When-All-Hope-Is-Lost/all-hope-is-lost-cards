## Do this now
**Find your average last spring frost date** from your state extension office (linked on your state guide card). Every time below counts from that date. Hardiness zone alone is not a planting date.

## Steps
1. **Count from your last frost date.** Mark it on a calendar and count back or forward (UNH Extension).
2. **Cool-season crops go in before the last frost:**
   - **Cabbage** transplants: about 4 weeks before last frost (UNH). Start seed indoors 4 to 6 weeks earlier.
   - **Onion** transplants: about 4 weeks before (UNH). Hardy crops, including onions, lettuce and cabbage, can go in 2 to 4 weeks before last frost once the soil is dry enough to work (CSU).
   - **Lettuce** transplants: 3 to 4 weeks before (UNH).
   - **Potato** seed pieces: 1 to 2 weeks before (UNH). CSU lists 45°F soil as the minimum.
   - **Carrot** seed: 0 to 2 weeks before last frost (CSU "semi-hardy"). Minimum soil temperature is 40°F.
3. **Warm-season crops go in after the last frost:**
   - **Beans:** sow after last frost (UNH).
   - **Sweet corn:** sow when the soil reaches 60°F (UNH).
   - **Tomato:** start seed indoors 6 to 8 weeks before transplanting; set out 1 to 2 weeks after last frost (UNH).
   - **Pepper:** start seed indoors 8 to 10 weeks before; set out 2 weeks after (UNH).
   - **Squash:** sow when the soil reaches 70°F, or transplant 2 weeks after last frost (UNH).
4. **Check soil temperature** at 4 inches deep in the morning if you can. CSU uses soil temperature as one of the best planting signals.
5. **Plant short-season crops in succession** (lettuce, beans, corn) every couple of weeks for a longer harvest (UNH marks these "S").
6. **Plant a fall crop.** CSU notes cole crops (cabbage, broccoli) do best seeded in midsummer for a fall harvest in many areas. Ask your extension office for local dates.

## If / then
- **If** a late frost is forecast after you plant tender crops **then** cover them overnight or replant. Tender crops don't tolerate frost (CSU).
- **If** you don't know your frost date **then** wait until nights stay above freezing and local nurseries put out tomato plants. Better, call the extension office.
- **If** you live where summers are very hot **then** plant cool-season crops for fall and winter and ask the extension office for the local calendar.

## Don't
- Don't set out tomatoes, peppers, squash or beans before the frost date.
- Don't plant in cold, wet soil. Seed rots.
- Don't plant potatoes from grocery-store potatoes if you can get certified seed potatoes. *(Common extension advice, not taken from the sources above; check locally.)*
