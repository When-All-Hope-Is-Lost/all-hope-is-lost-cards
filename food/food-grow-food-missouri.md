## Do this now
Plant the quickest crops your space and season allow, and **keep using food banks and SNAP** while you wait for a harvest.

## Steps
1. **Find your planting dates** in MU Extension's free *Vegetable Planting Calendar* (G6201), which has a Missouri regions map and a table of dates.
2. **Know your zone.** Look up your ZIP code on the USDA Plant Hardiness Zone Map (planthardiness.ars.usda.gov). Your local extension office can confirm yours.
3. **Start with quick, cheap crops from seed:** leafy greens and radishes in the cool seasons, and bush beans in the warm season. Check days-to-harvest on the seed packet.
4. **No yard or renting?** Grow in containers or buckets with drainage holes. Herbs and some greens grow on a windowsill.
5. **Plant again in fall** where the calendar lists a second set of dates.
6. **Save seeds** only from open-pollinated or heirloom plants (hybrids may not grow true). Dry them fully and store them cool, dry, and labeled.

## If / then
- **If** the soil is near old buildings, busy roads, or industrial land **then** grow in containers with bagged soil (lead and other contamination are possible), or get a soil test through MU Extension.
- **If** it's mid-summer **then** plant heat-tolerant crops or wait for the fall planting window.
- **If** you need seeds or soil **then** ask food banks, libraries (some have seed libraries), or your MU Extension county office.

## Don't
- Don't count on a garden for food in the first weeks. Bridge with food banks and SNAP.
- Don't plant on land you don't have permission to use.
- Don't eat produce grown in soil you suspect is contaminated until it's tested.

## Get help now
Hungry now: **2-1-1** or the food-today card.
