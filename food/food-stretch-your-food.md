## Do this now
**Use up what's already in your fridge, freezer, and cabinets first**, then plan the week's meals around cheap staples like beans, rice, oats, and frozen vegetables.

## Steps
1. **Take stock.** Look in the fridge, freezer, and cabinets, and plan meals that use those foods first. Eat fresh food before it goes bad.
2. **Know your food budget for the week,** then write a meal plan and a shopping list that fits it.
3. **Build meals on low-cost staples:** dried or canned beans, peas, and lentils (cheap protein, and dried beans keep a year or more), brown rice, old-fashioned oats, grits, eggs, and canned tuna in water.
4. **Bulk up meals** with beans and canned or frozen vegetables. They stretch a dish, add fiber and nutrients, and help you feel full.
5. **Shop smart:**
   - Eat before you go, and stick to your list.
   - Compare **unit prices** on the shelf tag.
   - Try **store brands**.
   - Buy produce in season and what's on sale.
   - Buy large bags of frozen vegetables. Frozen and canned fruit is similar in nutrition to fresh.
   - Look for day-old bread and in-store markdowns. Join the store's free loyalty program.
6. **Buy in bulk only what keeps** (whole grains, dried beans, frozen vegetables), and only as much as you'll use before it spoils.
7. **Handle leftovers safely:** refrigerate within **2 hours** (**1 hour** if it's above 90°F), in shallow containers. Eat within **3 to 4 days** or freeze. Reheat to **165°F**.

## If / then
- **If** the money runs out before the month does **then** use food pantries now (see the food-today card) and apply for SNAP. Don't wait until the cabinets are empty.
- **If** canned vegetables are salty or fruit is in heavy syrup **then** rinse them, or buy "no salt added" or "packed in juice" next time.
- **If** you have a windowsill **then** herbs and some greens are cheap to grow (see the garden card).

## Don't
- Don't let kids go without to save money. Ask a pantry, 211, or the school about free meals first.
- Don't eat leftovers that sat out longer than 2 hours (1 hour in the heat).
- Don't overbuy "deals" you'll end up throwing away.

## Get help now
**2-1-1** for food pantries and meals. SNAP: mydss.mo.gov/apply (Missouri) or fns.usda.gov/snap/state-directory. **988** if money stress is overwhelming.
