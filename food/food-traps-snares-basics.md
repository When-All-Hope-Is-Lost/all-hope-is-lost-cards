## Do this now
**Check your state's trapping rules first (see your state guide card). Many states restrict or ban snares and deadfalls, and trapping generally requires a license and an open season.**

## Steps
1. **Get the license and read the trapping rules** for your state, including legal trap types, trap-check intervals, and where you may set traps.
2. **Prefer legal, live-catch cage traps** where allowed. They avoid catching pets or protected animals by mistake.
3. **Set where animals travel**, such as trails, runs and feeding signs. ATP 3-50.21 explains that narrowing a trail toward the trap ("channelization") guides the animal in.
4. **Mask human scent:** the Army manual notes mammals avoid human scent; handling with mud-coated hands or letting gear weather helps.
5. **Use bait the animal already knows**, but not bait that is everywhere nearby.
6. **Check traps often**, as your state requires, and handle every catch with gloves (see wildlife-rabid-animals and wildlife-ticks).
7. **Cook all wild game thoroughly** and keep meat cold or preserve it (see food-smoking-curing-meat).

## If / then
- **If** you catch a pet, a protected species or the wrong animal **then** release it if you safely can, or contact the state agency.
- **If** an animal acts strangely (no fear, staggering, drooling) **then** don't touch or eat it; see wildlife-rabid-animals.

## Don't
- Don't set snares, deadfalls or body-gripping traps unless your state's rules allow that type, place and season. Survival needs do not override the law.
- Don't set traps where children or pets play.
- Don't eat an animal that looked sick.

## Get help now
Bites or scratches from wild animals: see a doctor; rabies is fatal once symptoms start. Rules: your state wildlife agency (state guide cards).
