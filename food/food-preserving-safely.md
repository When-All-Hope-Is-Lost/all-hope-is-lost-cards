## Do this now
**Use only tested recipes** (from the National Center for Home Food Preservation, USDA, or a university extension), and follow them exactly.

## Steps
1. **Low-acid foods** (vegetables, meats, poultry, seafood, most soups) **must be pressure-canned**, using a tested time and pressure for your altitude. A boiling-water canner isn't safe for them.
2. **Don't change a tested recipe:** keep the same ingredients, jar size, packing method, and processing time.
3. **Freezing and drying** are simpler, lower-risk ways to keep many foods. Follow tested guidance for them too.
4. **Label every jar** with what's in it and the date.

## If / then
- **If** a jar or can is **bulging, leaking, swollen, or damaged, spurts or foams when opened, or smells bad or looks moldy** **then** don't taste it. Throw it out carefully (CDC home-canning guidance).
- **If** anyone has **double or blurred vision, drooping eyelids, slurred speech, trouble swallowing or breathing, or muscle weakness** after eating home-preserved food **then** **call 911**. Botulism is a medical emergency. Symptoms usually start 12 to 36 hours after eating, sometimes after nausea, vomiting, or stomach pain.

## Don't
- Don't use old, untested, or social-media canning recipes.
- Don't "dry can" raw vegetables in the oven. NCHFP lists it as unsafe.
- Don't taste food from a suspicious jar "to check."

## Get help now
Botulism symptoms: **911**. Questions about a possible exposure: **Poison Help 1-800-222-1222**.
