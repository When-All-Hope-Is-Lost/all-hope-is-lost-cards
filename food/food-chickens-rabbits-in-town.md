## Do this now
**Check your city's rules and get any required permit before you buy animals.**

## Steps
1. **Check local rules first.** Many cities limit or ban backyard poultry and rabbits. Common rules include: a maximum number of hens (often tied to lot size), **no roosters**, an enclosed coop and run in the back yard, no free roaming, rodent-proof feed storage, a permit, and sometimes **no on-site slaughter or egg sales**. Ask your **city clerk** or animal-control office, and your local extension office, what applies to your address. Online city codes may not be the official version.
2. **Rabbits:** rules vary, and many cities don't mention them. Ask before you buy.
3. **Count the cost first:** a coop or hutch, feed, bedding, and vet care come before any eggs or meat, which take weeks to months.
4. **Stay healthy (CDC):**
   - Backyard poultry can carry *Salmonella* even when they look healthy and clean.
   - **Wash your hands for 20 seconds** after touching birds, eggs, or supplies.
   - Keep dedicated coop shoes, and keep birds and their supplies out of the house.
   - **Children under 5 shouldn't handle poultry.**
5. **Cook eggs** until the yolk and white are firm (egg dishes to 160°F).
6. **Buy chicks from hatcheries in the USDA National Poultry Improvement Plan (NPIP)**, as CDC recommends.

## If / then
- **If** you were counting on raising your own meat **then** check whether on-site slaughter is allowed where you live (many cities don't allow it).
- **If** someone at home is under 5, pregnant, older, or has a weak immune system **then** take extra precautions or reconsider (CDC).
- **If** birds get sick or several die suddenly **then** see the poultry-disease card and contact a vet or the state veterinarian. Bird flu is possible.

## Don't
- Don't kiss or snuggle poultry, and don't eat or drink around them (CDC).
- Don't keep animals you can't feed through the winter.
- Don't let birds roam where the city bans it.

## Get help now
Sick or dying birds: a vet, the Missouri State Veterinarian, or the USDA hotline (see the poultry-disease card). Food today: **2-1-1**.
