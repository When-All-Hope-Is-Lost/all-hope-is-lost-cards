## Do this now
**Heat meat to 160°F (poultry 165°F) before drying it. Drying alone does not make raw meat safe.**

## Steps
1. **Keep raw meat at 40°F or below** until you process it. USDA: use ground meat and poultry within 2 days, whole cuts within 3 to 5 days.
2. **Wild game needs extra care.** USDA notes venison can be heavily contaminated with fecal bacteria and is often held warm after the kill. Chill it fast.
3. **Cut thin strips, then heat to 160°F (meat) or 165°F (poultry)** by steaming or roasting, measured with a food thermometer.
4. **Dry at a steady 130 to 140°F** until dry, in a dehydrator, oven, or a controlled smoker.
5. **Curing salt (salt plus sodium nitrite) adds safety.** USDA cites a study where jerky made with a cure mix had more bacteria destroyed than jerky without it. Use only tested recipes for amounts (NCHFP, linked below).
6. **Store home-dried jerky 1 to 2 months** (USDA), in clean, dry, sealed containers kept off the ground and away from animals.

## If / then
- **If** you have no thermometer **then** cook meat until well done before drying, and eat dried meat sooner rather than later.
- **If** the dried meat smells off, shows mold, or got wet **then** throw it out.

## Don't
- Don't rely on marinade, salt rub or sun drying to make raw meat safe. USDA says marinating alone doesn't, and sun drying meat isn't recommended.
- Don't cold-smoke meat for storage without a tested curing procedure.
- Don't feed home-dried meat to infants, pregnant people or very sick people if you can avoid it.

## Get help now
Vomiting, bloody diarrhea, or signs of dehydration after eating preserved meat: see fa-diarrhea-oral-rehydration and get medical care. Botulism signs (blurred vision, drooping eyelids, trouble swallowing or breathing): **911**. USDA Meat and Poultry Hotline: 1-888-674-6854.
