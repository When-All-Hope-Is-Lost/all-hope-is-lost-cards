## Do this now
**Only eat a wild plant or mushroom if you're 100% sure what it is. When in doubt, throw it out.**

## Steps
1. **Identify every plant with at least two trusted sources** (for example, an MDC field guide plus a knowledgeable person). Learn the poisonous look-alikes for anything you pick.
2. **Wild mushrooms: only with in-person expert confirmation.** Some deadly mushrooms look like edible ones.
3. **Pick away from contamination:** avoid roadsides, rail lines, old industrial land, sprayed lawns and parks, flood debris, and sewage outfalls.
4. **Missouri conservation areas:** nuts, berries, fruits, edible wild greens, and mushrooms may be taken **for personal use only**. Taking wild greens is **not allowed on designated Natural Areas**, some areas ban collecting entirely, and you may not cut or destroy vegetation. Check the area's own rules.
5. **City parks, private land, and other public land** have their own rules. Get permission first.
6. **Wash everything well, and try only a small amount** of any new wild food at first.

## If / then
- **If** someone feels sick after eating a wild plant or mushroom **then** call **Poison Help: 1-800-222-1222** (or 911 if it's severe), and keep a sample of what they ate.

## Don't
- Don't rely on apps or "universal edibility tests" alone.
- Don't forage for greens on Missouri Natural Areas, or anywhere it's posted.
- Don't feed wild foods to children until you're certain of them.

## Get help now
Vomiting, confusion, or trouble breathing after eating a wild food: **911**. Questions: **1-800-222-1222**.
